## Content

The US specialty-crop farmworker context is the labour-side institutional and demographic substrate that the corpus has *named* (per `units/agaid-wsu-institute.md` L80 — *"The field guide does not yet have substantive US farmworker AI deployment coverage"*) but has *not unitised*. This unit closes G-176 substantively by surfacing the H-2A scale, regional labour contexts, and the organised-voice institutional landscape.

The Sullivan UCSC cluster is the analytical-voice anchor; this unit is the institutional-and-demographic anchor. **The two together** (Sullivan cluster analytical depth + this unit institutional breadth) cover more of the US farmworker labour landscape than either alone.

### H-2A temporary agricultural worker programme

The H-2A programme is the US Department of Labor's temporary agricultural worker visa programme. Per US DOL:
- **FY2024 H-2A visas issued**: 384,362 (the substantive scale figure).
- **Employer-paid transportation, housing, and wage determination**: H-2A employers must provide transportation from origin to worksite, housing, and pay the Adverse Effect Wage Rate (AEWR) — a wage floor that varies by state and occupation.
- **Programme growth**: H-2A has grown from ~50,000 visas per year in the early 2000s to ~384,000 in FY2024 — *substantial structural growth* over two decades.

The H-2A scale is the labour-cost structure that the agrifood automation push responds to. **California overtime regulation (2024)** + **H-2A AEWR increases** + **specialty-crop labour cost increase** are the three structural drivers of the automation push documented by Sullivan + Carolan + Fairbairn et al.

**AI deployment interaction with H-2A cost structure** is named in Sullivan unit but not unitised. The structural question: does automation *reduce* H-2A dependence, or does it *complement* H-2A (e.g. for tasks automation cannot do, like harvest) and *substitute* H-2A only for tasks automation can do (e.g. weeding)? Sullivan ethnography supports the latter reading — automation is targeted at H-2A-cost-reduction for solvable tasks (weeding) while leaving the unsolvable tasks (harvest) to farmworkers. **G-3XX (new): H-2A + AI deployment data at regional level.**

### Regional labour contexts

The Sullivan UCSC cluster is anchored in the **Salinas Valley** (Monterey County, California). The regional labour contexts that the corpus has *not* surfaced:

**Florida berry region.**
- **Production scale**: Florida strawberry + blueberry production substantial; Florida is among the top US states for both crops.
- **Labour demographics**: Mexican-origin farmworkers including **Mixteco and Triqui indigenous-language speakers** (Puebla / Oaxaca origin) — Mixteco and Triqui are not mutually intelligible with Spanish and require indigenous-language interpretation for labour-context outreach.
- **Institutional anchor**: **Farmworker Association of Florida (FWAF)** — Hawthorne FL + Apopka FL. Founded 1983. Hispanic Federation + Farmworker Association + coalition organising on pesticide exposure, heat stress, wage theft. (Verifying URL in deep phase.)
- **H-2A scale**: Florida has substantial H-2A scale; specific 2024 figures by Florida county not surfaced in scout.

**Washington apple region.**
- **Production scale**: Washington apples are the largest US apple crop; Yakima Valley is the primary production region.
- **Labour demographics**: Mexican-origin + Mixteco + Triqui indigenous-language speakers; H-2A scale substantial.
- **Institutional anchor**: **Familias Unidas por la Justicia (FUF)** — independent farmworker union founded in Skagit Valley WA; led the **Sakuma Brothers 2017 strike** — berry workers in Burlington WA. FUF is the *independent farmworker union* — not affiliated with UFW; the substantive independent farmworker organising of the past decade. (Verifying URL in deep phase.)
- **Yakima Valley labour context**: H-2A scale substantial; the *H-2A + AI deployment interaction* in Washington apples is named in scout as a substantive gap.

**California Central Valley (table grapes + others).**
- **Production scale**: California Central Valley produces ~99% of US table grapes; substantial stone fruit, citrus, nut production.
- **Labour demographics**: Mexican-origin farmworkers; substantial UFW legacy.
- **Institutional anchor**: **United Farm Workers (UFW)** + **UFW Foundation** — Dolores Huerta + Cesar Chavez legacy; Sacramento + Kern County + Coachella Valley. UFW has been the substantive US farmworker union organising since the 1960s; current organising on AI deployment + worker-safety + heat regulation.
- **California Rural Legal Assistance (CRLA)**: Founded 1966. Sacramento + multiple regional offices. CRLA is the substantive legal-aid organisation for rural California farmworkers. (Verifying URL in deep phase.)
- **H-2A + table grapes + AI deployment** interaction is named in scout as a substantive gap.

**Oregon (PCUN).**
- **Production scale**: Oregon berries + cherries + wine grapes + nursery stock.
- **Labour demographics**: Mexican-origin farmworkers; substantial indigenous-language speakers.
- **Institutional anchor**: **PCUN (Pineros y Campesinos Unidos del Noroeste / Northwest Tree Planters and Farmworkers United)** — founded 1985; Woodburn OR. PCUN is the substantive Oregon farmworker union; current organising on AI deployment + pesticide exposure + worker-safety.

**Georgia Vidalia region.**
- **Production scale**: Vidalia onions are the namesake Georgia crop; substantial sweet corn, watermelon, peach production.
- **Labour demographics**: Mexican-origin + Guatemalan-origin farmworkers; substantial H-2A scale.
- **Labour context**: H-2A + Georgia Vidalia + AI deployment interaction is named in scout as a substantive gap.

### The organised-voice institutional landscape

**Coalition of Immokalee Workers (CIW)** — Founded 1993; Immokalee FL. The substantive worker-led farmworker organisation in the US Southeast; operates the **Fair Food Program** (FFP) — a worker-driven social responsibility programme that has expanded from Florida tomatoes (2001) to strawberries, peppers, and other crops. The Fair Food Program is the substantive US example of farmworker-led labour standards enforcement; it is the *worker-side* complement to the consumer-facing USDA organic or fair-trade certifications. CIW + FFP + retail-participant agreements (Whole Foods, Walmart, McDonald's, Trader Joe's, etc.) are the substantive US farmworker labour-organisation institutional pattern. (https://ciw-online.org/)

**United Farm Workers (UFW) + UFW Foundation** — Founded 1962 by Cesar Chavez + Dolores Huerta + Filipino-American farmworkers (Aguirre / Itliong / Dolipas / Velasco). The historic US farmworker union; Sacramento + multiple regional offices. UFW Foundation is the 501(c)(3) arm; current organising on immigration, worker-safety, AI deployment, and pesticide exposure. UFW's role is *structural*: the historic Mexican-American + Filipino-American farmworker coalition that built farmworker labour rights as a substantive US institutional fact.

**PCUN (Pineros y Campesinos Unidos del Noroeste)** — Founded 1985; Woodburn OR. The substantive Oregon farmworker union; current organising on AI deployment + pesticide exposure + worker-safety + immigration.

**Familias Unidas por la Justicia (FUF)** — Founded 2013; Burlington WA + Skagit Valley. Independent farmworker union; led the **Sakuma Brothers 2017 strike** — berry workers in Burlington WA walked off the job demanding wage increases + working-condition improvements; the strike lasted ~7 months and ended with a partial settlement. FUF is the *independent farmworker union* — not affiliated with UFW; the substantive independent farmworker organising of the past decade.

**Farmworker Association of Florida (FWAF)** — Founded 1983; Hawthorne FL + Apopka FL. Hispanic Federation + Farmworker Association + coalition organising on pesticide exposure, heat stress, wage theft, and (per recent organising) AI deployment in Florida agriculture.

**California Rural Legal Assistance (CRLA)** — Founded 1966; Sacramento + multiple regional offices. The substantive legal-aid organisation for rural California farmworkers. CRLA's role is *legal*: individual + class-action representation on wage theft, housing, immigration, pesticide exposure, and labour-organisation rights. CRLA is the *legal infrastructure* of US farmworker labour rights.

### Three substantive analytical framings

**Framing 1: H-2A cost structure + automation push.** California overtime regulation (2024) + H-2A AEWR increases + specialty-crop labour cost increase = automation push. Sullivan documents that the automation push is targeted at solvable tasks (weeding); harvest remains unsolvable at commercial scale. The structural reading: automation *complements* H-2A rather than *replaces* it.

**Framing 2: Race-and-food California lineage.** Almaguer *Racial Fault Lines* UC Press 1994; Walker *Conquest of Bread* New Press 2004; Friedland et al. *Manufacturing Green Gold* CUP 1981. The historical-baseline analytical framing that places US agrifood labour in the context of California's racial fault lines. Per Sullivan cluster unit: *"The historical-baseline analytical framing that places US agrifood labour in the context of California's racial fault lines."*

**Framing 3: Organised-voice as counterweight.** Per the Carolan CSU cluster unit: *"What is not there: substantive sociological / labour-conditions / disability-and-recognition critique from US-based researchers."* The organised-voice layer (CIW, UFW, PCUN, FUF, FWAF, CRLA) is the *labour-side institutional voice* that complements the analytical critical-voice clusters (UCSC + CSU). The two layers together cover the US critical-voice landscape for AI and labour.
