## Content

The Carolan CSU ecosystem is the corpus's second-most-substantive US analytical cluster for agrifood AI, and is structurally complementary to the Sullivan-Guthman-Fairbairn-Mitchell UCSC cluster. Anchored at Colorado State University Sociology (Carolan as Professor; Co-Director of the Food Systems Institute), with cross-cluster reach to AI-LEAF (a USDA-NIFA AI Institute — institutional proximity but analytical distinction), the cluster's analytical position is *sociological* (the recognition dimension, the labour-and-disability dimension, the productive-affective politics of digital agriculture) rather than *ethnographic* (which is the UCSC cluster's position).

This is an ecosystem unit (per METHOD #6 four-layer pattern, Pillaud / Neethirajan / Mozilla precedent) — the cluster is *load-bearing* across multiple analytical voices, and reading them as a single ecosystem makes the cluster's analytical position load-bearing for the corpus.

### Four-layer ecosystem mapping (per METHOD #6)

**Layer 1 — Direct collaborators.** The cluster's core members:
- **Michael Carolan** (Professor of Sociology; Co-Director, Food Systems Institute, Colorado State University). 300+ peer-reviewed publications. The corpus's most-substantive *US-based sociological critical voice* on digital agriculture. Per corpus hygiene (substantive analytical contribution over citation volume): Carolan's work is included because he engages substantively with the labour, recognition, and disability dimensions of agrifood AI deployment — dimensions that mainstream agrifood AI reports (USDA AI Strategy, the AI Institutes' communications, vendor marketing, investment pitches) systematically avoid.
- **CSU Food Systems Institute members** — interdisciplinary food systems research hub at Colorado State University. Co-Director position for Carolan.
- **CSU Sociology Department graduate students and post-docs** — institutional training pipeline.
- **AI-LEAF Institute adjacency** — AI-LEAF is a USDA-NIFA AI Institute with CSU as a partner. Carolan's Food Systems Institute is institutionally *adjacent* to AI-LEAF's CSU partner work but analytically *distinct* — Carolan's work is critical-sociological, AI-LEAF's CSU work is technical / applied.

**Layer 2 — Institutional partners.** The cluster's institutional anchors:
- **Colorado State University** — Fort Collins, CO. Land-grant university (one of the 1862 institutions).
- **CSU Food Systems Institute** — interdisciplinary food systems research hub; Co-Director for Carolan.
- **AI-LEAF Institute** — USDA-NIFA AI Institute; partner with CSU. Adjacent (not overlapping) with Carolan's critical-sociological work.
- **Agriculture and Human Values** (Springer) — Carolan 2024 publication venue.
- **Sociologia Ruralis** (Wiley) — Carolan 2023 publication venue.
- **New Media & Society** (SAGE) — Carolan 2023 publication venue.

**Layer 3 — Industry / vendor counterparts.** The cluster's analytical posture is *against* the dominant agrifood AI industry framings:
- **Vendor productivity-and-efficiency framings** (Bayer, John Deere, Indigo Ag, AGCO) — Carolan's *"perilous promise of productivity"* (NMS 2023) is the *political-economy critique* of vendor productivity claims.
- **Federal agencies (USDA AI Strategy)** — workforce-readiness-and-ethics framings; Carolan's work does not engage in the workforce-readiness-and-ethics framing but does not duplicate it.
- **Academic research institutes (AgAID, AIIRA, AIFARMS, AI-LEAF)** — adopt-adapt-amplify / digital-twin / autonomous-farming framings; Carolan's work is institutionally adjacent to AI-LEAF but analytically distinct.
- **Land-grant deployment (Cooperative Extension)** — people-trust-coordination framings.

**Layer 4 — Framework positions.** The cluster's analytical framings are the load-bearing intellectual contribution:
- **(Dis)Ableism / labour / recognition** — Carolan's most distinctive analytical contribution. *"Agriculture 4.0 at the intersectionality of (Dis)Ableism, labor, and recognition"* (*Agriculture and Human Values* 2024) is the substantive paper that names how digital agriculture's recognition structures systematically exclude disabled workers, farmworkers with limited formal documentation, and care work. The (Dis)Ableism framing is *structurally distinctive* — most agrifood AI critical work does not engage with disability.
- **Happy objects and cruel quests** — *"Digital agriculture killjoy: Happy objects and cruel quests"* (*Sociologia Ruralis* 2023). Carolan names what he calls the "happy objects" framing of AI / robotics / data tools — the affective politics of digital agriculture — and the "cruel quests" that follow when these framings meet labour realities on farms.
- **Perilous promise of productivity** — *"The perilous promise of productivity: Affective politics"* (*New Media & Society* 2023). The *political-economy critique* of productivity claims in digital agriculture. The framing is substantive — it names the political economy of productivity claims without rejecting agrifood AI per se. (Per the Carolan unit: *"Carolan's framing of 'the perilous promise of productivity' is a political-economy critique, not a rejection of agrifood AI per se. Worth knowing because his work can be misread as anti-technology when it is anti-uncritical-adoption."*)

### Three substantive papers

**Paper 1: *Who and what gets recognized in digital agriculture: Agriculture 4.0 at the intersectionality of (Dis)Ableism, labor, and recognition*** — Carolan's most substantive recent US-engaged work. Engages with how digital agriculture's *recognition* structures (what counts, what is measured, whose work is visible) systematically exclude disabled workers, farmworkers with limited formal documentation, and care work. The (Dis)Ableism framing is structurally distinctive — most agrifood AI analytical work does not engage with disability. (Agriculture and Human Values, 2024, doi:10.1007/s10460-024-10560-9.)

**Paper 2: *Digital agriculture killjoy: Happy objects and cruel quests*** — A substantive critique of the affective politics of digital agriculture — what Carolan calls the "happy objects" framing of AI / robotics / data tools, and the "cruel quests" that follow when these framings meet labour realities on farms. (Sociologia Ruralis, 2023, doi:10.1111/soru.12398.)

**Paper 3: *The perilous promise of productivity: Affective politics*** — Engages with the productivity framing that dominates agrifood AI discourse. The "perilous promise" framing is substantive — it names the *political economy of productivity claims* in digital agriculture. (New Media & Society, 2023, doi:10.1177/14614448231174521.)

### What the cluster does NOT do

- **No IDSov engagement.** The Carolan CSU cluster does not engage with Indigenous Data Sovereignty frameworks (CARE Principles, IEEE 2890-2025, OCAP, NISR); IDSov is a parallel critical-voice cluster (Cornell Atkinson IDSov cluster; UCSC cluster does not engage IDSov substantively either).
- **No labour-organisation engagement.** Carolan's work is sociological-analytical; he does not engage UFCW / NFU / CIW / UFW as labour-side institutional voices. The labour-organisation engagement is the proposed `units/ufcw-nfu-clc-canada-labour-producers.md` layer.
- **No regional comparative scope.** Carolan's work is US-anchored; the labour-organisation / IDSov / labour-policy layers are international. The two clusters are complementary on labour, complementary on US scope, but not overlapping.

## Why it matters for talks

- **Carolan is the canonical US-based critical-voice researcher for agrifood AI sociology.** Talks that need substantive critical analytical content (academic, policy, or critical-civil-society audiences) should reference Carolan's framing.
- **The (Dis)Ableism framing is structurally distinctive.** Most agrifood AI critique focuses on smallholder / Indigenous / data-sovereignty concerns; Carolan adds labour and disability dimensions that are genuinely distinct.
- **Carolan's CSU position connects him institutionally to the AI-LEAF cluster; the proximity is worth knowing for talks that pair technical and critical framings.**
- **Ecosystem unit, not single-actor unit.** The cluster's analytical position is *not* the position of any single member; it is the position of the cluster. Reading the cluster together makes the analytical position load-bearing the way Pillaud / Neethirajan / Mozilla / Sullivan-Guthman-Fairbairn-Mitchell ecosystem units do.

## Critical context

- **Carolan is *not* a researcher who deploys AI.** He is a critical analyst. Treating his work as a counterweight to vendor / institute framings is appropriate; treating his work as a substitute for substantive technical work is not.
- **The 300+ publications indicator includes a wide range of topics** — food systems generally, not just digital agriculture. The digital agriculture subset is substantive but is a portion of broader work.
- **Carolan's framing of "the perilous promise of productivity" is a *political-economy critique*, not a rejection of agrifood AI per se.** Worth knowing because his work can be misread as anti-technology when it is anti-uncritical-adoption.
- **Carolan's positionality**: he is US-based but his work engages substantively with global food systems. The "US-based" classification in this unit is institutional-location; his analytical reach is broader.
- **The cluster does *not* count Carolan's work toward the v4 taxonomy's `actor-type: academic` quota in the same way as the AI Institutes** — Carolan's `claim-type: claim` rather than `example` reflects that his work is analytical rather than deployment-describing.
- **AI-LEAF adjacency is institutionally real but analytically distinct.** The proximity is worth knowing for talks that pair technical (AI-LEAF) and critical (Carolan) framings; it does not mean Carolan is part of AI-LEAF.
