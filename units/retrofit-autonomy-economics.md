## Content

The autonomy story farmers actually bought in 2025–2026 is not the autonomous tractor. It is the retrofit: grain-cart automation, tillage kits, and low-horsepower conversions on machines farmers already own. This unit assembles the economics of that layer — what it costs, who has deployed, and what the independent and farmer-side evidence says about whether it pays.

**AGCO PTx OutRun — the mixed-fleet grain-cart play.** The Fendt autonomy basic package (the interface enabling autonomous driving, e.g. with the PTx OutRun retrofit kit) became orderable on Fendt tractors in USA, Canada and Australia (3 September 2026). PTx OutRun applications now cover grain cart AND tillage. Trade-reported pricing (AgWeb December 2025 / DTN April 2026): grain cart ~$55,000 hardware + $15,000/yr; tillage ~$44,000–54,000 + $9,000/yr. Structurally notable: OutRun runs on Deere 8R/8000R tractors as well as Fendt — the mixed-fleet counter to Deere's equipment-locked autonomy. AGCO's Tech Day 2026 (5 October, Yorkville IL) demonstrated autonomy/AI/mixed-fleet operation; AGCO states a full-farm autonomy goal by 2030. Fendt markets its tractors as "already achieving Level 3 autonomy" by combining assistance functions — vendor framing, not a standard classification.

**Sabanto — the low-horsepower retrofit fleet.** Closed an oversubscribed Series B led by Leaps by Bayer (14 July 2026; with Sustainable Forward Capital, InnoVenture Iowa, Fulcrum Global Capital, DCVC, Yara). Trade-reported fleet: 200–300 retrofit kits running in the US, Canada and Australia; >20 at King Ranch, Texas, which joined Sabanto's dealer network (~July 2026). Kit price $70,000 (trade-reported, DTN April 2026), fitting 2015–2024 Kubota M5, Deere 5E/5M/6E, Fendt 700 Vario — modest machines, not 400-hp flagship tractors. Spring 2027: driverless planting support for planters with Precision Planting Gen 3 20/20 — the first move beyond tillage/mowing/seeding/grain-cart work. Also integrates with Verdant Robotics SharpShooter for driverless plant-level application. Sabanto's founder reports that farmers keep their workers and use autonomy to farm more acres — acreage expansion, not labour substitution.

**John Deere second-generation autonomy kit — deployed, tillage-only, undisclosed.** No unit count or acreage figure has ever been published (G-482). Third-party datapoints: a dealer's customer group ran 7 autonomous tractors over 13,000 acres in fall 2025; SAE/CES 2025 coverage reported ~50,000 autonomous acres with paid pilot customers — three orders of magnitude below See & Spray's 5M acres in the same year, the cleanest demonstration that "announcements exceed deployments" is true even at Deere, just in a different product line. Trade-reported pricing: ~$40,000–45,000 kit + ~$10,000/yr unlimited tillage acres. The largest announced US autonomous-tractor deployment — U.S. Sugar, 255,000 acres, 5 tractors (May–June 2026) — runs on third-party ASI Vehicle Automation Kit, not Deere's autonomy.

**The subscription-forever structure — the farmer-side critique.** An Illinois dealer (Jacksonville Journal-Courier): "$40,000-$45,000 to add autonomy to an AutoTrac-capable tractor PLUS ~$10,000/year subscription... whenever a product is introduced, Sloan always looks at costs and thinks that can't possibly pencil out." Economist Atallah notes no data exists on how subscription fees affect adoption by farm size (G-491) — and that service/rental models could erode the large-farm scale advantage that the whole precision-ag economics story has rested on.

**The repair-for-data trade.** The FTC–Deere right-to-repair settlement (sued January 2025; settled July 2026 with 10 years of FTC oversight) coexists with a private class settlement of ~$99M paying farmers ~$0.79/acre — farmer commentary: "three ears of corn per year per acre." Repair access itself requires a paid Deere software subscription ($195/machine/yr Pro Service; $4,995–5,995/yr fleet, per Wired's hands-on) that grants Deere a lifetime licence to farmer machine data. Named plaintiff Jared Wilson: "it's pretty pernicious that I have to give up all of my data... in exchange for repairing my machine." Wired also documents farmers using cracked grey-market software instead. This is the structural reason farmers hesitate: the deployed AI products carry recurring subscription and data-licensing terms disclosed only after purchase — and it quantifies the distrust the Canadian survey data records (88–89% of prairie producers; `units/row-crop-ai-independent-adoption-evidence.md`).

**The Purdue counter-model.** Purdue ag economists (Commodity Classic 2025): at $30/hr labour, "there's never a scenario where autonomy is better than having labor do the work." Sabanto's own published cost case ($18.88 vs $6.27/acre against conventional operations) counts investment but NOT labour — which is consistent with the founder's own observation that farmers keep their workers. The economic case for autonomy is currently unresolved and, where it matters most (subscription effects by farm size), data-free.

**FieldAI — the capital counterfactual.** FieldAI raised $405M at a $2B valuation (August 2025; Bezos Expeditions, NVIDIA NVentures, Khosla, Temasek; + Hyundai strategic investment February 2026) for GPS-free, map-free, embodiment-agnostic "Field Foundation Models" — agriculture listed as a target vertical, named deployments in construction, energy, security, manufacturing, and zero in agriculture (G-490). Roughly 4× the capital of any ag-robotics company, pointed at a possible bypass of the equipment-vendor lock-in pattern — or a failure to land in ag at all.

## Why it matters for talks

The 50,000-acres-vs-5,000,000-acres contrast (Deere autonomy vs Deere See & Spray, same company, same year) is the sharpest single statistic on autonomy hype. The repair-for-data trade — paying a subscription to repair a machine you own, in exchange for a lifetime data licence — is the most concrete illustration of the corpus's data-rights framework applied to row crops.

## Critical context

- Pricing figures ($55K OutRun, $70K Sabanto, $40–45K Deere kit, all subscription tiers) are trade-reported, not vendor-published price lists — treat as indicative, and verify against dealer quotes before citing as fact.
- The Nebraska OutRun harvest-speed account (one farm finishing harvest 20–25% faster) is explicitly a company narrative, not an independent trial.
- The ~$99M class settlement and $0.79/acre figure come from settlement-notice coverage (Straight Arrow News, 404 Media, Wired); the class settlement and the FTC injunctive settlement are separate instruments and should not be conflated. FTC injunctive relief is gated on reaching 50% of the dealer network.
- The Purdue $30/hr result is a model result under stated wage assumptions; it is not a measurement of realised farm outcomes.
