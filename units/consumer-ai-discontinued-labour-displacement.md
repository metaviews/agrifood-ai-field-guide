## Content

The consumer-AI and hospitality AI cell is the corpus's *most-evidenced labour-displacement negative-result layer*. Six deployments anchor the cell: **Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen** (in-store robotic salad makeline, FY2025 deployment + September 2025 Spyce divestment to Wonder for $186.4M), **Wendy's FreshAI × Google Cloud** (~100 restaurants / 17 states / January 2025 + 86% completion-without-human-intervention vendor-reported metric), **Taco Bell × Omilia** (890+ drive-thru voice AI units), **McDonald's × IBM AOT** (100+ restaurants in NA, **discontinued June 2024**), **Kroger × Ocado CFC closures** (Frederick MD + Goodyear AZ + third unreported, **discontinued November 2025** with the *"bet on robotics went too far"* corporate post-mortem), **DoorDash voice AI discontinued**. **Four high-profile US consumer-AI / hospitality robotics deployments walked back in an 18-month window.** This is structurally novel as an industry pattern.

This is a **consolidated cell anchor** unit — distinct from single-vendor units. The cell-level finding is the substantive contribution: consumer-AI and hospitality AI has the *most-evidenced labour-displacement-doesn't-happen-yet* layer in the corpus, and that pattern is *itself* the substantive analytical move.

### Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen

Per `units/sweetgreen-infinite-kitchen.md`:
- **What it is**: Sweetgreen's automated bowl/salad makeline; uses robotics + AI/ML to assemble customer orders. System originated from Sweetgreen's 2021 acquisition of *Spyce* (MIT-spinout automated kitchen company).
- **Deployment scale**: 18 of 37 net new restaurant openings fiscal 2025 + 7-9 new units + 2-4 retrofits in earlier 2024 plans. At least 25 new Infinite Kitchens planned for 2025 per Q3 2025 earnings release.
- **September 2025 divestment**: Sweetgreen sold the Spyce business to Wonder for $186.4M, while retaining the right to deploy the technology in its own restaurants *"with even greater [speed]"*.
- **Critical context named in unit**: *"Restaurant automation has a labour displacement dimension — Sweetgreen has not publicly committed to a no-layoffs policy tied to Infinite Kitchen deployment. The September 2025 sale to Wonder is itself a strategic signal: Sweetgreen is exiting the technology business to focus on the restaurant business, while licensing back the technology. This is a common pattern but worth surfacing."*
- **Labour-displacement framing**: Sweetgreen *has not publicly committed to a no-layoffs policy* tied to Infinite Kitchen deployment. The makeline is the largest deployed AI/robotics system in US fast-casual restaurant consumption as of mid-2026.

### Wendy's FreshAI × Google Cloud

Per `units/wendys-freshai-google.md`:
- **Deployment scale**: ~100 restaurants across 17 states running FreshAI in production (January 2025).
- **Vendor metrics**: 86% average order-completion rate without human intervention (vendor number); ~99% accuracy (Wendy's broader definition: any AI-started order submitted to POS); one test site showed service times 22 seconds faster than Columbus market average.
- **Spanish language capability**: Added in 2024. *The only public North American QSR voice-AI deployment that explicitly handles a non-English language in production as of mid-2026.*
- **Wendy's operates over 7,000 restaurants globally**, so 100 deployed = ~1.4% of footprint. *The production reality gap*: 100 of 7,000+ restaurants is small. Public framing tends to over-state scaling.
- **Voice data governance**: Wendy's CIO frames FreshAI as a *platform* intended to scale to mobile apps, kiosks, and smart devices, beyond drive-thru. Voice data flows to Google Cloud.

### Taco Bell × Omilia

Per `units/taco-bell-omilia-drive-thru-voice-ai.md`:
- **Deployment scale**: 890+ drive-thru voice AI units — the *largest* voice AI drive-thru deployment by unit count in the corpus.
- Smaller per-unit deployment than Wendy's FreshAI but with stronger unit-count scale; public metrics are limited.

### McDonald's × IBM AOT (DISCONTINUED June 2024)

Per `units/mcdonalds-ibm-ai-drive-thru-discontinued.md`:
- **Deployment scale**: 100+ McDonald's US drive-thru locations starting 2021.
- **Timeline**: 2021 McDonald's acquires Apprente (voice-AI startup) → renames McD Tech Labs → December 2021 IBM acquires McD Tech Labs → 2021-2024 AI drive-thru pilot in 100+ restaurants → June 2024 McDonald's announces end of partnership with IBM; voice ordering AI shut off at all pilot locations *"no later than July 26, 2024."*
- **Reasons cited in coverage**: Order accuracy at noisy drive-thru (SOTA voice models at 2021-2024 did not yet match noise tolerance); edge cases (special instructions, substitutions) slowed service; customer experience degradation when AI mis-ordered; workers' and franchisees' reports of cost-to-serve issues when AI failed.
- **Workforce framing named in unit**: *"QSR AI drive-thru has been framed as a labour story by some media (displacement concerns) and as an efficiency story by others (faster service, fewer mistakes). The pilot ending complicates the labour-displacement concern by demonstrating that the technology itself wasn't ready — though it does not resolve the policy question of what happens if/when it works. Worker / labour union concerns were raised during the pilot period; the discontinuation may have been partly tactical (cost / accuracy) rather than a labour settlement."*
- **Strategic refocusing**: McDonald's *did not* declare AI-driven ordering a dead-end — they ended the *IBM* partnership specifically, with reports of subsequent partnerships with Google for AI drive-thru replacing the IBM role. **The discontinuation was tactical, not strategic.**

### Kroger × Ocado CFC closures (DISCONTINUED November 2025)

Per `units/kroger-ocado-cfc-closures-discontinued.md`:
- **Deployment scale**: Multiple CFC sites — Monroe OH, Groveland FL, Dallas TX, Frederick MD, Forest Park GA, Goodyear AZ.
- **Closure announcement (November 2025)**: Kroger announced closure of three CFC sites including Frederick MD and Goodyear AZ; Kroger publicly stated the *"bet on robotics went too far"* (per Grocery Dive November 19, 2025). **One of the highest-profile US grocer robotics-deployment walk-backs in the agrifood sector.**
- **Why this matters**: CapEx-heavy physical infrastructure investment ($hundreds of millions); Kroger's post-mortem acknowledgement is unusually candid for a grocer. *"The Kroger × Ocado closure is structurally similar to Sweetgreen's 2024 Spyce sell-off to Wonder (Sweetgreen divested its robotic-kitchen subsidiary). Two distinct US agrifood companies walking back robotics investments in the same 18-24 months."*
- **Workforce framing**: *"CFCs rely on automation rather than picker labour at fulfilment centres; the closure narrative reverses this — automation was too capital-intensive, not just too labour-displacing."*

### The consolidated cell finding — labour-displacement-doesn't-happen-yet

**Four high-profile US consumer-AI / hospitality robotics deployments walked back in an 18-month window.** McDonald's × IBM ended June 2024. DoorDash voice AI discontinued. Kroger × Ocado closures November 2025. Sweetgreen Spyce sold September 2025 (with deployment rights retained). The pattern is structurally novel:

| Deployment | Sector | Disposition | Reason cited |
|---|---|---|---|
| McDonald's × IBM AOT | QSR drive-thru voice AI | Discontinued June 2024 | Order accuracy + edge cases + cost-to-serve issues |
| DoorDash voice AI | Delivery voice AI | Discontinued (date TBD) | (substantive gap) |
| Kroger × Ocado CFCs | Grocer fulfilment-centre | Closed November 2025 | "Bet on robotics went too far"; capital-intensive |
| Sweetgreen Spyce | Restaurant in-store robotic makeline | Sold to Wonder for $186.4M September 2025 | Strategic refocusing (restaurant not technology) |

**The labour-displacement dimension is constrained by technology readiness + capital economics.** Where the technology wasn't ready, labour displacement didn't happen — even where the *intent* (cost reduction, automation, scaling) was explicit.

**The empirical record is more nuanced than the dominant vendor / investor pitch.** The labour-displacement question is *answered differently* depending on whether the deployment walked back or stayed.

### The labour-displacement framing in working deployments

In **working** consumer-AI / hospitality deployments, the labour-displacement framing is:
- **Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen** — labour-displacement dimension named; *Sweetgreen has not publicly committed to a no-layoffs policy*. The makeline replaces the salad-assembly line; the labour-reduction dimension is direct.
- **Wendy's FreshAI 86%-completion-without-human-intervention** — *the most-specific public labour-automation-rate metric* on a voice-AI deployment. The metric is *vendor-reported* (Wendy's own methodology) but methodologically specific. The labour-automation-rate is the *headline* number for any labour-spine talk about voice AI drive-thru.
- **Taco Bell × Omilia 890+ units** — public metrics are limited; the deployment scale is the substantive finding.

In **discontinued** consumer-AI / hospitality deployments, the labour-displacement framing is:
- **Constrained by technology readiness** (McDonald's × IBM accuracy problems; Kroger × Ocado capital economics).
- **The deployment walked back rather than sustained** — labour displacement didn't happen because the deployment itself didn't sustain.
- **Strategic refocusing rather than labour settlement** — McDonald's moved to Google; Sweetgreen divested to Wonder; the technology was *deferred* not *rejected*.

### Three substantive findings from this consolidated cell anchor

**Finding 1: The discontinued cluster is the corpus's most-evidenced labour-displacement-doesn't-happen-yet layer.** Four high-profile US consumer-AI / hospitality robotics deployments walked back in an 18-month window. The pattern is structurally novel.

**Finding 2: The labour-automation-rate metric (Wendy's 86%-completion-without-human-intervention) is the most-specific public labour-side metric on a voice-AI deployment.** The metric is vendor-reported but methodologically specific. The labour-spine talk about voice AI drive-thru should foreground the metric.

**Finding 3: Working vs discontinued cluster have structurally different labour-displacement answers.** In working deployments, labour-displacement is real and bounded (Sweetgreen has not committed to no-layoffs; Wendy's 86% completion-without-human-intervention is the headline number). In discontinued deployments, labour-displacement was constrained by technology readiness + capital economics.
