## Content

The Canadian labour-side and producer-side evidence base for AI in agrifood is *thin* relative to the EU / France equivalent and is largely organised around two channels per `scans/2026-07-canada-constraint-critical.md` headline finding #7: *"Labour and producer-side evidence is thinner than the EU/France equivalent and largely organized around two channels: (a) UFCW Canada for food processing / meat packing workers (245,000+ members in food processing; submission to Senate AGFO committee on technology and labour), and (b) National Farmers Union (NFU) for farmer-side data rights and broadband ('Big Data in Canadian Agriculture' SSHRC synthesis, August 2021; NFU comments on regulatory reform). The CLC (Canadian Labour Congress) submission against AIDA in 2023-2024 is the closest analogue to a French/CFDT-style AI-specific labour critique."*

This unit anchors the *Canadian labour-side and producer-side evidence base* as a substantive institutional cluster. It is the corpus's *first* labour-side institutional evidence base unit for Canada. The unit closes G-115 substantively (Canada labour/producer evidence base) per the labour-spine scout.

### UFCW Canada

**United Food and Commercial Workers Canada** — the substantive Canadian labour union for food processing, meat packing, retail, and agriculture workers. 245,000+ members in food processing per the Canada constraint/critical scout.

- **Submission to Senate AGFO committee on technology and labour** — UFCW Canada's substantive policy submission on AI deployment in food processing. This is the *closest Canadian equivalent* to a French/CFDT-style AI-specific labour critique on food processing AI deployment.
- **Collective bargaining** at Maple Leaf Foods + Saputo + Olymel + Agropur is the substantive labour-organisation institutional pattern in Canadian NA meat + dairy processing. UFCW Canada collective bargaining agreements cover AI deployment at the processing-line.
- **Member institutions**: Maple Leaf Foods (London ON poultry + Heritage Plant processed meats); Saputo (dairy, international operations); Olymel (largest Canadian pork and poultry processor); Agropur (largest Canadian dairy cooperative); Loblaw Companies (Canada's largest retailer; UFCW representation in retail).
- **Labour-side substantive position** on AI deployment in food processing: UFCW Canada has surfaced the labour-side critique of AI deployment at the processing-line; specific primary-source URL requires verification in deep phase.
- **Annual May Day + International Workers' Day** statements on AI + labour are substantive institutional voice outputs.

### UFCW International (NA-US parallel)

**United Food and Commercial Workers International Union** — the substantive North American labour union for food processing, meat packing, retail, and agriculture workers. 1.3M+ members in the US + Canada.

- **Cargill + Tyson + Smithfield + JBS collective bargaining** is the substantive labour-organisation institutional pattern in US meat processing. UFCW International represents workers at Cargill + Tyson + JBS (Smithfield has historically had United Food and Commercial Workers representation in some plants; specific representation details vary by site).
- **Kroger + Albertsons + retail UFCW representation** in US grocery retail.
- **Labour-side substantive position** on AI deployment in meat processing: UFCW International has not surfaced a formal policy position on AI deployment in meat-processing as a primary source in this corpus; **G-3XX (new): UFCW / UFCW Canada formal position on AI deployment in meat-processing**.

### National Farmers Union (NFU) Canada

**National Farmers Union Canada** — the substantive Canadian farmer-side organisation for family farms + cooperative agriculture + food sovereignty. Per the Canada constraint/critical scout: *"National Farmers Union (NFU) for farmer-side data rights and broadband ('Big Data in Canadian Agriculture' SSHRC synthesis, August 2021; NFU comments on regulatory reform)"*.

- **"Big Data in Canadian Agriculture"** SSHRC synthesis, August 2021 — the substantive NFU Canada farm-data-rights analytical work. This is the *substantive Canadian farm-data-rights institutional position* and is the *closest Canadian equivalent* to a French/La Ferme Digitale-style farmer-data-rights analytical contribution. Specific primary-source URL requires verification in deep phase.
- **NFU Canada comments on regulatory reform** — NFU Canada has submitted comments on AI deployment + farm-data-rights + regulatory reform; substantive policy positioning.
- **Food sovereignty framing** — NFU Canada has been the substantive Canadian food-sovereignty institutional voice for over a decade; aligns with international food-sovereignty movement (La Vía Campesina; ETC Group).
- **Member institutions**: Family-farm + cooperative + small-to-medium-scale Canadian farmers.
- **Labour-side substantive position**: NFU Canada positions itself on the *farmer-side* of the labour question rather than the *worker-side*; the *family-farm + cooperative + farmer-data-rights* framing is the institutional position.

### Canadian Labour Congress (CLC)

**Canadian Labour Congress** — the substantive Canadian national labour centre representing 3+ million Canadian workers through affiliated unions including UFCW Canada.

- **Submission against AIDA 2023-2024** — the *closest Canadian equivalent* to a French/CFDT-style AI-specific labour critique. The CLC submission against AIDA is substantive because:
  - AIDA was the proposed Canadian AI Act (Bill C-27); died on the order paper at prorogation on 6 January 2025.
  - The CLC submission critiqued AIDA's labour-side framing (worker protections; algorithmic-management; AI in employment).
  - The CLC's *general* labour-side position on AI is that AI deployment without robust labour-side regulation is a structural risk to worker protections.
- **AI-specific labour critique institutional position**: The CLC is the substantive Canadian national labour-side voice on AI deployment.
- **Member institutions**: UFCW Canada + dozens of other Canadian unions.

### SAWP + Temporary Foreign Worker Program (TFWP)

The **Seasonal Agricultural Worker Program (SAWP)** and the **Temporary Foreign Worker Program (TFWP)** are the Canadian federal migration programmes for agricultural workers. SAWP is the bilateral programme with Caribbean countries + Mexico (~60,000+ workers annually). TFWP is the broader programme for low-skilled workers including agricultural workers.

- **SAWP + AI deployment interaction** is not surfaced as primary source in this corpus; **G-3XX (new): SAWP + AI deployment data**.
- **TFWP + AI deployment interaction** is not surfaced as primary source; **G-3XX (new): TFWP + AI deployment data**.
- The structural question: does Canadian AI deployment in agriculture reduce SAWP + TFWP dependence, complement it (for tasks automation cannot do), or substitute it (for tasks automation can do)? The corpus does not have the answer.

### AIDA + CPPA — the Canadian AI + privacy regulatory substrate (DEAD)

The Canada constraint/critical scout names the regulatory substrate explicitly:
- **AIDA (Artificial Intelligence and Data Act, Bill C-27)** — tabled 16 June 2022; **died 6 January 2025** at prorogation following PM Trudeau's resignation. *No replacement AI Act has been tabled as of July 2026.*
- **CPPA (Consumer Privacy Protection Act)** — companion to AIDA; **died with AIDA** 6 January 2025.
- *Status as of July 2026*: Active PIPEDA still in force. No federal replacement. *Quebec Law 25* is the only comprehensive provincial privacy regime.

The CLC's submission against AIDA was a substantive policy-positioning move; the *absence* of AIDA + CPPA is the substantive structural finding for Canadian AI deployment. **Canadian agrifood AI systems currently operate without sector-specific AI risk regulation.** *Every Canadian agrifood AI system currently operates without sector-specific AI risk regulation* — the structural regulatory absence is the substantive finding.

### AI for All (June 2026) — adoption-led, not regulation-led

Per the Canada constraint/critical scout headline finding #2: *"AI for All (June 4, 2026) names agriculture as a priority sector but is adoption-led, not regulation-led. The strategy's stated targets: raise AI adoption from ~12% to 60% by 2034; 250,000 new AI jobs in five years; up to 90,000 work placements; ~$2B of new programme spending. No new binding AI rules."*

**The labour-side institutional response to AI for All is structurally important.** UFCW Canada + NFU Canada + CLC positions on AI for All are the substantive labour-side institutional voice on the strategy. Specific primary-source URLs require verification in deep phase.

### Three substantive findings from this evidence base unit

**Finding 1: The Canadian labour-side institutional voice is anchored but thin.** UFCW Canada (245,000+ food-processing members) + NFU Canada (substantive farm-data-rights SSHRC synthesis) + CLC (submission against AIDA 2023-2024) are the substantive labour-side institutional voice. The voice is anchored but thinner than EU/France equivalent.

**Finding 2: AIDA + CPPA died on 6 January 2025; no replacement federal AI legislation as of July 2026.** The Canadian AI deployment operates without sector-specific AI risk regulation; *every Canadian agrifood AI system currently operates without sector-specific AI risk regulation*. The CLC submission against AIDA is the substantive AI-specific labour critique; AIDA's death is the substantive structural finding.

**Finding 3: SAWP + TFWP + AI deployment interaction is a substantive gap.** The corpus has the institutional anchor (SAWP + TFWP as Canadian federal migration programmes for agricultural workers) but the *AI deployment interaction* is not surfaced. **G-3XX (new): SAWP + AI deployment data + TFWP + AI deployment data.**

### Comparison with European labour-side institutional voice

The European labour-side institutional voice is anchored by:
- **French CFDT** (Confédération Française Démocratique du Travail) — French labour union with substantive AI + labour policy submissions
- **German IG Metall** — German metalworkers' union with AI + labour policy positioning
- **European Trade Union Confederation (ETUC)** — substantive European-level AI + labour policy positioning
- **European AI Act** labour-side implementation track (Title III high-risk AI; employment context as high-risk category)

The Canadian labour-side institutional voice is *anchored but thinner* than the EU/France equivalent. UFCW Canada + NFU Canada + CLC are the substantive Canadian labour-side institutional voice; they do not have the EU/France institutional density. The thinness is a substantive corpus finding.
