# Agroecology ↔ open source: correlation structure (cross-repo note)

Last reviewed: 2026-08-12
Method: mapping scan — reads existing field-guide agroecology-relevant units against the open-source idiom; not a discovery scan for new units. Produced by the G-OSA-24 cycle in `opensource-agrifood/`. This note feeds a correlation-structure reading into the field guide's protected-cultivation / openness axis; it does not replace any existing unit.

## Why this note exists

The field guide has substantive agroecology content — `units/open-source-ai-agrifood-quantitative-panel.md` names agroecology as the AI-deployment contrast case; `units/canadian-indigenous-data-sovereignty-agrifood.md` carries the Ruder-Wittman data-governance critical voice and Wittman's *Land governance for agroecology* (Elementa 2022); the EU institutional/funder scan names the agroecology partnership as a Horizon Europe Cluster 6 co-funded partnership. But the field guide does not yet have a unit or note that asks the question this project is built around: **when does "open source" strengthen agroecology's farmer/peasant-agency and anti-enclosure goals, and when does it dilute or misfire?** This note is the start of that answer, drawn from the G-OSA-24 correlation scan.

## Headline (from G-OSA-24 scan)

The correlation between agroecology and open source is **sub-domain-dependent, not uniform**. Four shapes, strongest to weakest:

1. **Open-source seed as a deliberate bridge** — OSSI pledge, OSSL licence, Bioleft (Argentina, participatory seed commons + open data) all explicitly marry a commons/open-source idiom to seed biology as an agroecological anti-enclosure tool. The Sievers-Glotzbach et al. (2020) Seed Commons criteria (collective responsibility, protection from enclosure, collective polycentric management, knowledge sharing; CC BY 4.0) are the closest conceptual bridge between a commons/open-source idiom and agroecology's seed-sovereignty current. **Tension:** OSSI pledge has uptake without enforcement; OSSL has contractual teeth with no located enforcement case (this project's G-OSA-21); the pledge-vs-licence trade-off is the live sub-question.

2. **Open-source machinery/tool design as a structural but fragile bridge** — L'Atelier Paysan (France, SCIC cooperative, 2009–2026, **judicially liquidated April 2026**; successor structures Communs Paysans + Soudons, fermes! attempting to protect the design assets from commercial appropriation) is the strongest located case of farmer-governed open hardware for agroecology; Farm Hack (US/UK, FAO agroecology knowledge hub record, peer design-sharing, candidate) is the looser decentralized counterpart; Open Source Ecology (GPL/CC-BY-SA tractor/machinery set) is the open-hardware engine but not agroecology-specific; Think Oggún is the inverse (an "open system" manufacturing model with a 5-year paid licence fee). **Tension:** the L'Atelier Paysan liquidation is the fragility finding — governance-model strength does not solve funding/maintenance (this project's G-OSA-08); whether the design commons survives the liquidation intact, and under what terms, is unresolved as of 2026-08-12.

3. **Open-source digital farm infrastructure as a partial, contested bridge** — REGOSH's Open Agroecology Lab (Argentina, farmOS + SurveyStack + open sensing collaborations, co-designed data governance, Shuttleworth-funded, activity documented 2018–2023, **post-2023 activity unverified**) is the strongest located case of an open-source *digital* infrastructure deliberately built for agroecological knowledge systematization; LiteFarm and farmOS extend the thread. **Tension:** IPES-Food's "Head in the Cloud" (Feb 2026) frames digital agriculture as an enclosure vector — "over 70% of agrifood innovations were digitally delivered," platforms "standardize and digitize farming knowledge" — and names commons, gift economies, and open-source frameworks as the counter-current, but situates open-source farm software *inside* a critique of digital enclosure, not as the dominant force. The correlation is real at the design layer; whether it delivers knowledge sovereignty at the deployment layer is unresolved.

4. **Institutional agroecology does not speak "open source"** — The AGROECOLOGY European Partnership (Horizon Europe Cluster 6, three co-funded calls closed 2024–2026, agroecology living labs + research infrastructures, member-state co-funding) surfaces **no open-source, commons, or licensing framing** in its public materials; the framing is living labs, research infrastructures, value-chain/policy assessment, genetic diversity, farmer empowerment — cooperative and participatory methods, but not "open source" as a term or instrument. FAO's scaling-up paper (2020) names "open source data and technology" as one element, but FAO's 10-element framework and HLPE do not center open source. **This absence is itself evidence:** the institutional agroecology current does not naturally speak "open source"; the two movements overlap at practice and knowledge-exchange but not at vocabulary or funding instrument.

## A fifth axis that the correlation does not bridge

**Knowledge sovereignty / Indigenous and peasant knowledge overlaps with open-source commons but is not expressed in open-source licensing terms.** The field guide's `units/canadian-indigenous-data-sovereignty-agrifood.md` is the substantive unit here: CARE Principles, OCAP®, FNDGS, IEEE 2890-2025, NISR, IQ — these are data-governance and knowledge-sovereignty frameworks that *do not map onto* open-source licensing vocabulary (free, open, forkability, commons). Agroecology's vocabulary (food sovereignty, peasant, territory, La Via Campesina) similarly does not map onto open-source vocabulary. Potato Park's customary biocultural governance (this project's record) is a distinct commons shape from OSSI/OSSL. The correlation between agroecology's knowledge-sovereignty current and open-source's forkability/commons current is **not resolved by anything in the record** — Prager (2021) "open source agriculture" is the closest general-frame reference and is not agroecology-specific.

## What the field guide already has that maps to this correlation

- `units/open-source-ai-agrifood-quantitative-panel.md` — agroecology is the AI-deployment contrast case in the quantitative panel; the panel's farmer-agency / smallholder frame is the closest field-guide anchor to agroecology's social-movement current.
- `units/canadian-indigenous-data-sovereignty-agrifood.md` — Ruder-Wittman critical voice (data justice with agri-food movements, *Big Data & Society* 2025; *Journal of Peasant Studies* 2025; *Journal of Rural Studies* 2026 with Duncan et al.); Wittman's *Land governance for agroecology* (Elementa 2022) and *Food sovereignty* model (One Earth 2023). This is the field guide's strongest agroecology-adjacent analytical voice, but it is framed as Indigenous data sovereignty, not as an agroecology↔open-source correlation.
- `units/open-source-in-agrifood-framework.md` — the A Growing Culture "Open Source Ethos" framing (Jan 2023) explicitly bridges software open source, scholarly open access, biological open-pollination, and traditional agricultural seed-saving — this is the field guide's existing conceptual bridge between open source and agricultural heritage, but it is framed as heritage/ethos, not as a sub-domain-correlation map.
- `units/potato-park.md` — customary biocultural commons; the distinct-commons-shape anchor for the knowledge-sovereignty axis.
- `units/bioleft.md` — the Argentine participatory seed commons + open data case; the strongest located bridge between a Latin American agroecological current and an open-source idiom.
- `units/latelier-paysan.md` — the farmer-governed open-hardware case; the structural-but-fragile bridge.
- EU institutional/funder scan (`scans/2026-07-eu-institutional-funder-substrate.md`) — names the agroecology partnership as a Horizon Europe Cluster 6 co-funded partnership.

## What the field guide does NOT yet have

- No unit or note that frames the agroecology↔open-source correlation as a sub-domain-dependent structure (seed strong, machinery fragile, digital contested, institutional vocabulary absent).
- No reference to the IPES-Food "Head in the Cloud" (Feb 2026) critique of digital-ag enclosure and its naming of commons/open-source/gift-economy frameworks as counter-current — this is the most current critical anchor on the digital-infrastructure side of the correlation.
- No reference to the AGROECOLOGY Partnership's *absence* of open-source framing as evidence that institutional agroecology does not speak "open source."
- No naming of the vocabulary-mismatch tension — agroecology's "food sovereignty / peasant / territory" vs open-source's "free / open / forkability / commons" — as a substantive framing question for the field guide (this maps onto the field guide's existing Q-33 / openness-discipline work).
