## Quote / Position

> "The agriculture industry is faced with the challenges of time, costs, constrained operational windows, and a dwindling labor force. Autonomy will become an integral part of farmers' operations everywhere... We believe that Autonomy and Automation technologies are fundamental to the future of farming. They cover all farmer segments and drive real world benefits."

## Context

This verbatim-quote entry is from Derek Neilson, President of Agriculture at CNH Industrial, at the December 2022 CNH Industrial Tech Day. The quote is the *canonical US equipment-vendor articulation* that autonomy is the *strategic response* to agricultural labour decline.

Worth knowing because:
- The quote is from a *named equipment-vendor executive* (CNH Industrial President Agriculture), not from an analyst or industry commentator.
- The quote names the *labour-substitution-as-driver-of-automation* framing explicitly: *"a dwindling labor force"* + *"Autonomy will become an integral part of farmers' operations everywhere"*.
- The quote is *commercially positioned*: it is from a CNH Industrial Tech Day event where the company was announcing its autonomous portfolio (Driver Assist Harvest, Driverless Tillage, Case IH Trident 5550 with Raven Autonomy). The framing supports the *vendor commercial positioning* of autonomy as the strategic direction.

Per the CNH-Raven unit: *"CNH framing (Derek Neilson, President Agriculture, December 2022 Tech Day): 'The agriculture industry is faced with the challenges of time, costs, constrained operational windows, and a dwindling labor force. Autonomy will become an integral part of farmers' operations everywhere... We believe that Autonomy and Automation technologies are fundamental to the future of farming. They cover all farmer segments and drive real world benefits.'"*

The quote is structurally distinct from the Korean state-cluster framing (30%-by-2027 quantitative adoption target): CNH Neilson frames labour-substitution as *vendor-led organic deployment*; Korean state-cluster frames labour-substitution as *state-led quantitative policy lever*. The labour-spine talk should foreground the *state-led vs organic* structural inversion.
