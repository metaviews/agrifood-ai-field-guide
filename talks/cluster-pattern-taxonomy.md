# Cluster-Pattern Taxonomy — How countries shape their agrifood AI cluster

**Status.** Promoted from `talks/archetypes/06-regional-cluster-comparison.md` because six regional deepening cycles (LAC, Brazilian beef + Chile-Canada, Argentine beef + Brazilian seed, MENA + Lebanon, Spain + North Africa, Spanish cooperatives) surfaced a cluster-pattern vocabulary that has outgrown its host document. The substantive finding that emerged across cycles — *cluster-patterns are plural; cluster patterns layer; some cluster patterns (cluster-with-three-structures) repeat across regions with structurally-distinct substrates* — needs a canonical home separate from any single archetype.

**Refreshed 15 November 2026.** The September 2026 EU architecture cycle and China primary-document cycle (plus the November 2026 refresh) are folded in: the EU-level architecture pattern enters as a named candidate (§1, §3.5), the China pattern acquires its six-component primary-source structure (§1, §2), and the *labour-substitution-via-state-cluster* candidate (§3.4) receives a disposition note against the new China-side evidence. Cycle detail: `talks/eu-china-cycles-2026-09.md`.

**Why this exists.** The field guide's regional-cluster comparison asks: *do countries with similar demographic imperatives (ageing-farm populations; rural labour decline; food-security anxiety) operationalise AI differently?* The answer across the corpus's six regional cycles: yes — and the operational shape of the cluster produces different outcomes for *who controls the data*, *who captures the value*, *what counts as deployment-of-record*, and *what's the deployment-shape critique*.

**How to use this doc.** Treat the cluster-pattern table as a query target, not a list. When building a regional talk, query the row that matches your region and follow the cross-references into units + scans + contested claims. When building a comparative talk across regions, query the cluster-pattern-name column to find all regions that operate the same pattern.

## 1. Cluster-pattern name vocabulary

| Pattern name | Definition |
|---|---|
| **NA-equipment-vendor** | Agricultural AI deployment led by industrial-machinery majors (tractors, drones, sensors) operating proprietary vendor platforms. Most visible at scale in NA-US; replicated where global majors sell. |
| **Cooperative-governance (NL pattern)** | Non-profit cooperative data-rights infrastructure where the data and benefits flow back to the farmer. Cultural substrate required: cooperative tradition (Netherlands), Federal-state + cooperative-bank presence. |
| **State-vendor hybrid + provincial autonomy** | State-led policy framing + vendor autonomy at the operational level + provincial-level variation. Substrate: state-aligned with vendor autonomy operationally; provinces drive variation. **September 2026 structure (primary-document cycle):** six components — (1) state-directed umbrella with named instruments (No. 1 Central Document → MARA Action Plan → Digital Village Plan → 数据要素× → MIIT tiering; AI inside existing instruments, no standalone strategy); (2) state-farm and state-enterprise deployment vehicles (Beidahuang, Sinograin, COFCO); (3) provincial variation as the implementation unit; (4) state-adjacent research infrastructure that writes the standards and holds the platforms (CAU, CAAS, Sinograin Chengdu); (5) a closed commercial platform layer underneath ("data islands" as the state's own framing); (6) hardware-OEM export as the international channel. The state instrumented what it owns before it instrumented what farmers do. |
| **Multi-layer institutional substrate with dispersed instruments and thin sectoral representation (EU pattern — candidate)** | The EU-level architecture formulation from the September 2026 cycle: a funder layer with no agrifood-AI programme; an industry-voice layer split by interest (CEMA has an AI dossier, Copa-Cogeca does not); a strategy layer that names agri-food but does not fund it (Apply AI flagship unbudgeted); a regulatory layer that regulates machinery, not farming intelligence (Annex I Section B sectoral route); a market-creation layer that has not transacted (CRCF, zero certificates); and zero agrifood seats in AI governance (178-entry Advisory Forum register screened in full). The distinction from the US and China is in *architecture*, not *layering*. Status: candidate with one cycle plus one refresh behind it; confirmation test is whether another institutional architecture (WAGRI, AgriStack, IICA-convened LAC) shows the same dispersion-without-constituency structure. Sits *above* the member-state cooperative-governance and state-trade-promotion sub-patterns, which remain valid at their level. |
| **State-DPI substrate + private vendor layers** | National state-developed digital public infrastructure (DPI) as substrate (farmer registry, plot registry, crop-sown registry; e-Mitra chatbot); private vendor layers build on top. Cascade across state agriculture departments. |
| **Equipment-vendor industrial automation** | Industrial-machinery majors (motorcycle engines, boats, robotics) apply competence to agriculture. Substrate: industrial-machinery vendor concentration; state-side regulatory framing. |
| **State-anchored cluster programme** | State-led cluster anchoring + multi-site innovation valleys + multi-vendor participation; vendor participation secondary to state anchorage. Strong quantitative policy levers. |
| **Multilateral-institutional convening (LAC pattern)** | IICA + IDB + CAF + Bayer + PROCISUR + U of Córdoba + AWS as convening substrate. Distinct from state-DPI substrate. |
| **Israeli venture-funded agritech-startup cluster** | Mature Israeli national ecosystem (750+ companies; 150+ startups; 33 funding rounds >$1M annually); venture-funded corporate-vendor deployment. |
| **State-strategy dual-mode** | State federal agency operating both (a) standards-setter mode (institutional governance framework / international-standards certification) and (b) deployment-of-record mode (specific AI deployment). Cultural-stewardship dimension distinct. |
| **State-corporate + state-strategy hybrid** | Multinational state-affiliated corporation (world's largest phosphate / world's largest fertilizer / etc.) operating AI deployment within national state strategy. |
| **Startup-ecosystem-emerging-expansion** | Single-agri startup with regional-VC funding; multi-source-data integration; expansion scope to regional + sub-continental scope. |
| **Cooperative-federation-institutional-substrate + emerging-single-coop deployment** | Multi-sector cooperative-federation (worker cooperatives since 1956; €11B+ revenue; 70,000+ workers) + emerging individual-cooperative AI deployment on open-innovation substrate. |
| **Cross-border cluster pattern** | Multi-country cluster spanning counter-season production + genetic-tooling flow + multinational-corporate-pipelined deployment. |

## 3. Cross-region cluster-pattern observations

Three observations have emerged from six regional cycles:

### 3.1 Cluster patterns are layered-mix, not single-pattern

The LAC, MENA, and EU-cluster-pattern rows all observe *layered-mix* structure: multiple operative cluster patterns operating within the same regional frame. *Three-region observation of layered-mix structure*.

### 3.2 Cluster-with-three-structures is a cross-region cluster pattern (substantive finding)

Two regional cases observe the cluster-with-three-structures pattern:

| Tier | Brazilian seed AI | Spanish cooperative AI |
|---|---|---|
| Tier-1: peer-reviewed-research-led | Sangjan 2025 cited 24; Tedeschi 2025 PMC cited 20; Stupar 2024 PMC | García-Lafuente 2026 BFJ; Ciruela-Lorenzo 2020 cited 248; Santos 2024 cited 72; Arévalo-Royo 2025 cited 25; Sadjadi 2023 cited 81 |
| **Tier-2** | **Multinational-corporate-pipelined** (Bayer / Syngenta / BASF / Corteva Brazil) | **Mondragón institutional-federation-anchor** (federation of worker cooperatives since 1956; €11.213B 2024; 70,000+ workers) |
| Tier-3: substantially-empty at deployment-of-record | Empty at Brazilian-origin-corporate-vendor | Emerging-single-coop deployment (COVAP Reto Innovación Abierta 2025) + Andalusian Cooperativas Agroalimentarias |

*The same cluster-with-three-structures pattern operates across two regional contexts with structurally-distinct Tier-2 substrates.* This is corpus-valuable; the next-cycle question is whether the same pattern operates across additional regional contexts (Italian agritech, Egyptian agritech, etc.).

### 3.3 Cluster-with-state-substrate as substantive pattern

Argentine beef AI (SENASA + SIGSA + World Bank financing) is the corpus's first cluster-with-state-substrate example distinct from cluster-with-state-strategy (UAE ADAFSA dual-mode). Worth surfacing in any talk framing state-led agricultural AI — *state-driven* and *state-strategy* are *not* synonyms.

### 3.4 Cluster-pattern-taxonomy candidate (surfaced by AI and Labour cycle, July 2026)

**Candidate name.** *labour-substitution-via-state-cluster*

**Definition.** State-led quantitative policy levers for agricultural AI adoption that target labour-substitution outcomes explicitly (e.g. Korea 30%-by-2027 smart-farming adoption; Japan industrial-automation heritage + WAGRI state-stewarded DPI; Chinese WAICO multilateral posture).

**Substantive distinction.** Distinct from:
- **cluster-with-state-substrate** (Argentine beef AI — SENASA + SIGSA + World Bank financing; the state provides the regulatory substrate but does not target labour-substitution).
- **cluster-with-state-strategy** (UAE ADAFSA — federal agency operating both standards-setter mode and deployment-of-record mode; the state strategy is dual-mode but not labour-substitution-targeted).
- **equipment-vendor industrial automation** (Deere / CNH / AGCO organic deployment; the state is not the substitution driver).

**Load-bearing for the labour spine.** If the Korean 30%-by-2027 + Japanese industrial-automation-heritage + Chinese WAICO multilateral posture substantiates this candidate, then *labour-substitution-via-state-cluster* becomes a cluster-pattern-taxonomy entry that the labour-spine talk can use to distinguish state-led labour-substitution from organic vendor-led deployment.

**Source.** `units/korean-state-cluster-labour-substitution.md`; `scans/2026-07-ai-and-labour.md` §5.

**Status.** Candidate observation; not yet elevated to confirmed cluster pattern. A future cycle that substantiates it through additional cluster-pattern data may elevate it to a substantive entry.

**Disposition note (15 November 2026, against the September China cycle evidence).** The China primary-document cycle supplied new state-cluster labour evidence but it *complicates rather than confirms* the candidate as formulated. The Chinese substitutions run through different machinery than a statutory adoption target: Muyuan replaces repetitious barn work wholesale inside engineered environments; XAG's drone labour shifts into service-provider fleets rather than displacing farm workers; Beidahuang's unmanned-farm labour reductions (50.3%/51.5%, self-reported) sit inside state-farm land consolidation; and MARA's own promoted-technology catalogue documents land structure and infrastructure, not AI capability, as the binding precondition. **The emerging reading: state-led labour substitution has at least two distinct mechanisms — statutory-adoption-target (Korea) and land-structure-and-state-estate (China) — with Japan's industrial-automation heritage a third, vendor-mediated case.** The candidate stays open; confirmation would require deciding whether one pattern name covers all three mechanisms or whether the taxonomy needs a mechanism dimension. Evidence: `talks/eu-china-cycles-2026-09.md` finding CN-7; `units/china-ground-robotics-unmanned-farms.md`, `units/china-state-farms-provincial-deployment.md`, `units/muyuan-smart-pig-farming.md`; `talks/archetypes/08-ai-and-labour.md` segment 6.

### 3.5 EU-level architecture candidate (surfaced by the EU architecture cycle, September 2026)

**Candidate name.** *Multi-layer institutional substrate with dispersed instruments and thin sectoral representation.*

**Definition and components.** See §1. The full formulation, funding figures, corrected regulatory dates and the governance-absence evidence are in `scans/2026-09-eu-substrate-deepening.md` §5-§6 and `talks/eu-china-cycles-2026-09.md` findings EU-1..EU-8.

**Relation to existing vocabulary.** This is the corpus's first pattern formulated at a *supranational* level rather than a national one, and it does not replace the member-state patterns (cooperative-governance NL, state-trade-promotion Spain, cooperative-federation Spain) — it is the layer above them, and its defining claim is precisely that the upper layer lacks the sectoral constituency the lower layers have. It also corrects the July funder scan's framing: the EU is not "the most multi-layered" of the US/EU/China trio so much as the most *instrument-dispersed and procedural*, and dispersion has a measurable cost (no actor can point to "the EU's agrifood AI policy" because there isn't one; the decision calendar in `units/eu-agrifood-ai-decision-calendar.md` is that cost made visible).

**Status.** Candidate; one cycle plus the November 2026 refresh behind it. Confirmation test named in §1: does another institutional architecture (Japan's WAGRI-centred substrate, India's AgriStack, the IICA-convened LAC layer) show the same dispersion-without-constituency structure, or is instrument dispersion specific to supranational governance? Until then it is a formulation with evidence, not a typology entry.
