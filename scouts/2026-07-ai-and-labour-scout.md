# AI and Labour Scout — July 2026

**Status.** Scouting-only pass. **No edits to the repo beyond this scout file.** Field guide material is in the existing scans and units cited above; this scout reconnoitres what's substantively covered and what's missing for an *AI and labour* spine cycle.

**Purpose.** The existing field guide covers labour as a tag (worker-conditions in frontmatter) and as a critical-voice side-finding (Sullivan "zero percent"; Lely succession; CNH Neilson "a dwindling labor force"). It does **not** have labour as the analytical spine of any scan, scan-tail finding, or unit set. Per `talks/README.md` Status section, "AI and labour / succession" is named as the next-iteration spine candidate with anchor units lely-astronaut + naio-technologies + quotes/torrie-jeff-lely-succession — partial surfacing. This scout reconnoitres across seven tracks to test whether the corpus has the substrate to make the spine load-bearing.

**Method.** Recon across seven tracks in parallel (on-farm robotics labour pattern; specialty-crop labour pattern; food-processing labour pattern; consumer-AI labour pattern; critical-voice labour lineage; labour-policy / state-cluster labour-substitution; migration / seasonal labour data). For each track: (1) what existing corpus units cover it; (2) what primary sources name the labour dimension explicitly; (3) what the substantive absences are. **Per METHOD #13 negative-finding tolerance**: where a track returns substantively thin, that thinness is named and tracked — it is not backfilled with adjacent material.

**Cutoff.** Active in the last 24 months (July 2024 – July 2026). Older material (Lely 1992 origin, Sullivan ethnography 2021-2025, Sullivan intellectual lineage 1981-2025, Torrie 2018 quote) included only when load-bearing.

**Note on bias.** Vendor materials are dominant in the on-farm robotics and food-processing strata; critical voices are concentrated in the specialty-crop and critical-voice strata; labour-policy material is concentrated in the Canadian constraint/critical scout and the EU regulatory substrate scan. The cycle's analytical task is to read these together, not to harmonise them.

## 0. Headline findings (TL;DR)

1. **The labour material in the corpus is *substantial* but *distributed*, not concentrated.** The corpus has ~30 units with explicit labour-side content (frontmatter `purpose: worker-conditions`, `critical-voice: worker-conditions`, or substantive labour discussion in body). They cluster in six strata — on-farm robotics, specialty-crop AI, food-processing AI, consumer-AI / discontinued cluster, critical voices, and labour-policy / succession / demographic — but no single scan or cycle has read them together as a *labour spine*. **Reading them together is the cycle's contribution.**

2. **The labour-displacement *empirical record* is mixed and structurally interesting.** Where automation is widely deployed (Lely 50,000 robots / 3M cows milked daily / 30-year history; Deere 5M+ acres See & Spray 2025; CNH Driverless Tillage / Driver Assist Harvest; Sweetgreen 18 net new Infinite Kitchen restaurants FY2025), the labour displacement is real but bounded. Where automation is *announced* but not deployed at scale (specialty-crop harvest; Consumer-AI voice-ordering; grocer fulfilment-centre robotics), the labour displacement is constrained by technology limits — Sullivan's "zero percent" head lettuce, McDonald's IBM AOT discontinued June 2024, Kroger Ocado closures November 2025 ("bet on robotics went too far"), DoorDash voice discontinued. **The discontinued cluster is the corpus's most-evidenced labour-displacement-doesn't-happen-yet layer.** It is currently scattered across four units; reading them together is the structural move.

3. **The labour-monitoring / surveillance dimension is named in only one unit (Cargill CarVe) and is a substantive gap.** CarVe's critical-context section names *"the same camera that estimates yield can also be used to monitor individual worker pace, technique, breaks"*. The Tyson × AWS and Smithfield vision systems have the same dual-use structure; their units do not name it. Wendy's FreshAI × Google Cloud's 86%-completion-without-human-intervention metric has a labour-automation-rate dimension not foregrounded. The Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen makeline has an explicit labour-displacement framing ("Sweetgreen has not publicly committed to a no-layoffs policy tied to Infinite Kitchen deployment") but it is buried in critical context rather than elevated. **G-3XX (new): labour-monitoring / surveillance dual-use of agrifood computer vision systems.**

4. **The critical-voice labour lineage is *rich but unconsolidated*.** Sullivan (UC Santa Cruz, PhD candidate) is the proximate voice, but the analytical foundation is Guthman (UCSC, *The Problem with Solutions* UC Press 2024; *Agricultural and Food Citizenship*; *Agrarian Dreams*), Fairbairn + Kish + Guthman (*Pitching Agri-Food Tech*, *Journal of Cultural Economy* 2022), Don Mitchell (*Taylorism Comes to the Fields*, *Economic Geography* 2023), Friedland + Barton + Thomas (*Manufacturing Green Gold* CUP 1981), and Fairbairn et al. (*Nature Food* 2025 — "Digital Agriculture Will Perpetuate Injustice Unless Led from the Grassroots"). Carolan (CSU; *Agriculture and Human Values* 2024 on (Dis)Ableism/labour/recognition; *Sociologia Ruralis* 2023 "killjoy" framing; *New Media & Society* 2023 "perilous promise of productivity") is the parallel US-side voice. **None of these are unitised as ecosystem maps** (per METHOD #6 four-layer ecosystem-mapping pattern). The Pillaud / Neethirajan / Mozilla precedent shows ecosystem-mapping is the methodology that makes substantive analytical voices load-bearing. **Sullivan-Guthman-Fairbairn-Mitchell UCSC ecosystem unit + Carolan CSU ecosystem unit are the cycle's two anchor unit candidates.**

5. **The labour-side organised-voice is *thin and vendor-adjacent* in the corpus.** Producers (5 quotes): Nelson (NA-US, data reluctance), Donkers (NL, vendor fragmentation), van den Bosch (NL, JoinData), Torrie (NA-Canada, Lely succession), Fratantuono (BE, Naïo vineyard). **No farmworker quote, no food-processing-worker quote, no H-2A / migrant-worker voice, no union-side quote.** Sullivan unit names a future `quotes/farmworkers/` category but it has not been built. The Sullivan SSRC essay contains verbatim farmworker focus-group quotes and verbatim roboticist quotes that the cycle should land. **G-3XX (new): farmworker voice in `quotes/farmworkers/` category.**

6. **The labour-policy / succession / demographic layer is regional and well-anchored but not unified.** Canada: AI for All strategy (June 2026) names agriculture as priority sector but is adoption-led not regulation-led; AIDA / Bill C-27 dead on prorogation January 6 2025; UFCW Canada + NFU Canada + CLC as labour-side institutional voices. Korea: 30%-by-2027 smart-farming adoption target (state-led labour-substitution). Japan: WAGRI state-stewarded DPI substrate; Yamaha Fazer RMAX industrial-automation heritage. EU: CNH Neilson framing; EU AI Act labour-side implementation track is thin in corpus. **The Korean state-cluster labour-substitution dimension is the strongest state-led labour-substitution corpus anchor** (30%-by-2027 + ioCrops + Daedong + RDA Smart Farm Innovation Valley) and warrants its own unit.

7. **Migration / seasonal labour data is structurally thin across the corpus.** H-2A visa programme scale, SAWP (Canada), Bracero legacy, COCONA equivalents in LAC, intra-EU seasonal labour, SSA smallholder labour are all under-surfaced in the agrifood AI context. The CSA / Olds College / AI-LEAF US side acknowledges "the field guide does not yet have substantive US farmworker AI deployment coverage" (agaid-wsu-institute.md L80 — gap G-013-adjacent, but distinct). **G-3XX (new): migration / seasonal labour + AI agrifood deployment — substantive under-surface across all regional scans.**

8. **The labour spine's structural inversions are the analytically distinctive contribution.** Four inversions the cycle should land:
    - (a) **Harvest vs weeding split** (Sullivan "zero percent" head lettuce; Advanced Farm Technologies / FarmWise Vulcan / Carbon Robotics LaserWeeder commercial scale; Harvest CROO prototype-only). Commercial deployment where easy; prototype-only where hard.
    - (b) **Succession vs displacement** (Lely / Torrie / Naïo "small farm / specialty crop" framing vs Sullivan / Carolan labour-conditions-and-recognition framing). Same technology, opposite framings — both real.
    - (c) **State-led vs organic labour-substitution** (Korea 30%-by-2027 vs Deere / CNH / AGCO organic equipment-vendor deployment). The labour question is *the same question* but the substitution pattern is structurally different.
    - (d) **Discontinued cluster as labour-displacement negative result** (Sweetgreen / McDonald's / Kroger / DoorDash). Where the technology wasn't ready, labour displacement didn't happen. This is the *empirical record* — it is more nuanced than the dominant vendor / investor pitch.

9. **The Canadian anchor is the right regional emphasis for a follow-on cycle, not for this one.** Per user direction, this cycle is global. Canadian labour material (UFCW Canada 245,000+ food-processing members; NFU Canada; CLC AIDA critique; AIDA dead; AI for All agriculture as priority sector) is *substantively* anchored and ready for a future Canadian AI-and-labour regional cycle — but that cycle is a follow-on, not this one.

10. **Architecture recommendation (see §11):** The cycle is **substantively justified** — the recon across 7 tracks returns substantial material in all 7. Phase 2 should fire with the architecture: (i) one substantive analytical scan reading the labour pattern across all six strata; (ii) two ecosystem-mapping units (Sullivan-Guthman-Fairbairn-Mitchell UCSC cluster + Carolan CSU cluster); (iii) four cluster-anchor / single-actor units (US specialty-crop farmworker context; NA meat-processing labour-displacement; consumer-AI discontinued labour-displacement negative-result; Korean state-cluster labour-substitution); (iv) one Canadian labour-side institutional unit (UFCW / NFU / CLC) — even though the cycle is global, the Canadian institutional voice layer is *substantively the most-anchored* labour-side material in the corpus and deserves a dedicated unit; (v) four new quote entries including the new `quotes/farmworkers/` category; (vi) 8-10 new G-NNN IDs and 2-3 new C-NNN IDs in registries.

## 8. Cross-track observations

### 8.1 Four structural inversions

The labour-spine's analytically distinctive contribution is the **structural inversions** — same technology, opposite empirical outcomes:
- **(a) Harvest vs weeding split** — specialty-crop AI has reached commercial scale for *weeding* (Advanced Farm Technologies / FarmWise / Carbon Robotics) but not for *harvest* (Sullivan "zero percent" head lettuce; Harvest CROO decade-plus prototype). The labour-displacement question is *answered differently* for the same agrifood sector depending on which task is automated.
- **(b) Succession vs displacement** — same technology, opposite framings. Lely Astronaut deployment is framed as *family-farm succession* (Torrie) by farmers and as *work-life balance* by vendors (Cargill Lely) — both real. Naïo Technologies deployment is framed as *labour-cost reduction* by vendors and as *small-farmer viability* by French cooperative discourse (Pillaud framing). The labour-spine must hold both framings without collapsing either.
- **(c) State-led vs organic labour-substitution** — Korea 30%-by-2027 + Japan WAGRI vs Deere / CNH / AGCO organic equipment-vendor deployment. The labour-substitution *question* is the same — who works on farms in 2035 — but the substitution *pattern* is structurally different (state-led + quantitative target vs vendor-led + organic deployment). **Cluster-pattern-taxonomy candidate**: *labour-substitution-via-state-cluster* as a substantive cluster-pattern distinction.
- **(d) Discontinued cluster as labour-displacement negative result** — Sweetgreen / McDonald's / Kroger / DoorDash discontinued cluster. Where the technology wasn't ready, labour displacement didn't happen — even where the *intent* (cost reduction, automation, scaling) was explicit. The empirical record is more nuanced than the dominant vendor / investor pitch.

### 8.2 Two structural absences (substantive)

- **Labour-monitoring / surveillance dual-use** — Cargill CarVe explicitly names the dual-use; Tyson × AWS / Smithfield / Wendy's FreshAI / Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen deployments have the same structural feature but their units do not name it. The labour-spine must surface the surveillance dimension as a substantive analytical move.
- **Labour-displacement quantification** — Lely 50,000 robots is concrete deployed scale; what does the workforce look like at a 50,000-robot-deployment scale? Pre/post employment data is structurally thin across the corpus. Same for Deere 5M+ acres See & Spray; CNH Driverless Tillage / Driver Assist Harvest.

### 8.3 Two structural features (substantive)

- **Labour-side organised voice is thin** — UFCW / UFCW Canada / NFU Canada / CLC / CIW / UFW / PCUN are *institutions* with substantial primary-source materials (collective bargaining agreements; policy submissions; organising campaigns) that the corpus has not surfaced. The labour-spine needs the organised-voice layer to balance the vendor / critical-voice layer.
- **Labour-voice verbatim quotes are thin** — the corpus has 5 producer voices (Nelson, Donkers, van den Bosch, Torrie, Fratantuono) and *no* farmworker / food-processing-worker / H-2A / union-side voice. Sullivan unit's named `quotes/farmworkers/` category is the structural fix.
