## 1. The question

G-484 asked whether the Arkansas seed-bank finding (low-sensitivity spot spraying →
escaped weeds → soil seed bank) reproduces beyond one state, one crop (soybean), one
prototype machine generation — and the deep-dive scan flagged that the Iowa State
43.9–90.6% savings range was secondary-sourced (case-studies.ai) and the ISU original had
to be located before corpus citation.

## 2. What was located

### 2.1 The ISU original — located and read at primary (resolves the flagged task)

**Houser D, Darr M, Huffman R (2024). "Precision Spraying Technology." Iowa State
University Extension, Integrated Crop Management News, August 22, 2024.**
https://crops.extension.iastate.edu/cropnews/2024/08/precision-spraying-technology

- A **field-scale demonstration** (not a replicated small-plot trial) of a **commercial**
  John Deere See & Spray Ultimate on five conventionally managed soybean fields, 415
  acres, Boone and Story counties, Iowa, in the wet spring of 2024.
- Per-field post-emergence product savings: 87.2% (Field 1, 110.7 ac, vertical-till),
  43.9% (Field 2, 71.9 ac), 71.2% (Field 3, 104.7 ac), 90.6% (Field 4, 37.1 ac), 87.6%
  (Field 5, 90.1 ac). **Average 76%**; ~$6,500 saved ($15.7/ac); 4,700 gallons of tank
  mix saved. The three lowest-weed-pressure fields averaged 88.4% ($18.1/ac).
- **Driver: initial weed pressure and pre-emergence residual herbicide performance.**
  Field 2 saved least (43.9%) because heavy spring rain broke down the residual program
  and heavy weed pressure would normally warrant a broadcast pass.
- Drone imagery and post-application scouting confirmed the machine correctly identified
  and sprayed emerged weeds; fields stayed clean through canopy formation.
- Scope limits: one season, one location, a demonstration (savings measured, not a
  multi-year seed-bank measurement). Republication-with-credit permission stated by ISU
  Extension (quotable with attribution; not a full open licence).

The 43.9–90.6% range is now citable to the ISU primary, not the secondary aggregator.

### 2.2 The Arkansas seed-bank finding — now peer-reviewed (upgrades V1 → V2)

**Avent TH, Norsworthy JK, Butts TR, Drescher Gerson L, Nalley LL, Vazquez AR (2026).
"Weed population impacts using targeted herbicide applications with a precision sprayer
in soybean over a three-year period." *Weed Technology* 40(e9), 1–10.
DOI 10.1017/wet.2025.10056** (open access).

- The peer-reviewed version of the Arkansas Agricultural Experiment Station news release
  the deep-dive scan cited. Three-year experiment from May 3, 2022, Northeast Research and
  Extension Center, Keiser, Arkansas; broadcast vs targeted at the highest and lowest
  See & Spray sensitivities (Blue River/Deere test machine).
- **Seed bank:** no increase over three years under broadcast or targeted-at-highest-
  sensitivity. At the **lowest sensitivity**, weed density rose 867 → 2,870 (Year 2) →
  11,300 plants/ha (Year 3); Palmer amaranth germination after three seasons increased
  783 (broadcast), 1,077 (highest), 2,246 (lowest) plants/m². The "280%/yr" news-release
  figure is the low-sensitivity escape-and-seed consequence.
- **Economics:** targeted applications saved $43.22–129.19/ha relative to broadcast
  post-emergence cost of $227.22/ha; targeted all-herbicide programs cost $140.89/ha
  (highest sensitivity) and $118.57/ha (lowest).
- Scope limits: still **one location** (Keiser, AR), soybean, prototype/test machine.

### 2.3 A multi-state, two-year replicated trial — AR + MS (addresses G-484's multi-state requirement for savings/control)

**Avent TH, Norsworthy JK, Patzoldt WL, Schwartz-Lazaro LM, Houston MM, Butts TR,
Vazquez AR (2024). "Comparing herbicide application methods with See & Spray™ technology
in soybean." *Weed Technology* 38:e74. DOI 10.1017/wet.2024.70.**

- Two years, **two locations: Keiser, Arkansas AND Greenville, Mississippi**;
  glyphosate/glufosinate/dicamba-resistant soybean; PRE residual + early-post dicamba +
  mid-post glufosinate, comparing broadcast, mixed, and targeted-all programs.
- **Targeted sprays saved 28.4–62.4% of post-emergence herbicide**, with weed control
  within ≤1% of broadcast (overall control ≥93%; every program ended the season at ≥98%
  control per the secondary summary); soybean injury reduced by at most 5 percentage
  points, with no yield increase at harvest.
- This is the multi-state, replicated savings-and-control evidence G-484 asked for — but
  it measures savings and control, not the multi-year seed-bank consequence.

### 2.4 Supporting evidence

- **"Targeted herbicide cost requirements for adoption of See & Spray technology and ways
  to improve area sprayed."** *Agrosystems, Geosciences & Environment*.
  DOI 10.1002/agg2.70324. Non-residual herbicide savings ranged 0–94% by weed density;
  cites Avent et al. 2024's 28–62% range. The adoption-economics counterpart.
- **WSSA 2025 annual-meeting abstracts** (cotton, Palmer amaranth): spot-spray-only (no
  residual) savings were much lower (10–30% corn, 12–24% soybean) than two-pass programs
  with residual herbicides (35–45% corn); late-season escape targeting (spot-spray + hand
  removal) was very effective at reducing seed-bank addition. Conference abstract (V1);
  reinforces that residual herbicides and escape management drive both savings and
  seed-bank outcomes.

## 3. Resolution

- **The ISU-original flag — resolved.** The 43.9–90.6% range is primary-citable (ISU
  Extension, Houser/Darr/Huffman, August 2024, commercial-machine field demonstration).
- **G-484 — partially resolved.** Savings and control are now documented in a
  **multi-state (AR+MS), two-year, replicated, peer-reviewed** trial (Avent et al. 2024:
  28.4–62.4%, control comparable to broadcast), and the seed-bank consequence is now
  **peer-reviewed** (Avent et al. 2026). The remaining gap is specific: the multi-year
  **seed-bank** consequence at low sensitivity is documented at **one location** (Keiser,
  AR, prototype machine, soybean). Whether **commercial machines at grower settings** in
  other states and crops reproduce the seed-bank increase is not established by a
  multi-state replicated seed-bank study. The ISU commercial-machine demonstration (one
  season) kept fields clean through canopy but did not measure multi-year seed bank.
  → G-495.
- **C-362 — refined and strengthened.** The "~50% herbicide reduction" figure holds as an
  average over a management-sensitive distribution, now anchored by three independent
  university sources: ISU commercial-machine demo (43.9–90.6%, weed-pressure-driven),
  AR+MS two-year replicated (28.4–62.4%, control comparable), and AR three-year
  (low-sensitivity seed-bank consequence). Savings depend on initial weed pressure,
  residual-herbicide performance, and sensitivity setting; the maximum-savings (low
  sensitivity) setting carries a documented seed-bank cost that erodes savings over
  years. The figure is not a machine property.

## 4. Verification posture

The ISU article was read in full at primary (V2-equivalent for its demonstration claim,
with the demonstration scope limits noted). The two *Weed Technology* papers are
peer-reviewed independent replications (V2); DOI, volume, authors and abstract/results
confirmed at Cambridge Core (abstract-level read; full-PDF read of Avent 2026 is open
access and available for deeper verification). The adoption-economics paper
(agg2.70324) and the WSSA abstract are supporting (V1–V2). The honest residual is the
single-location scope of the seed-bank finding (G-495), not the verification tier of the
located sources.
