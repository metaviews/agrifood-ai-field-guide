# EU Substrate Deepening — September 2026

**Status.** Author-facing internal scan. Cycle-document for the **EU architecture cycle** — the twelve units that close the thirteen open leads the two July 2026 EU substrate scans left behind. First field-guide cycle since the AI and Labour wave of late July, and the corpus's first September update.

**Builds on:**
- `scans/2026-07-eu-institutional-funder-substrate.md` (the funder / institutional layer, July 2026 — this scan closes its seven leads)
- `scans/2026-07-eu-regulatory-substrate.md` (the regulatory layer, July 2026 — this scan closes its five leads and corrects its timeline)
- `scans/2026-07-regional-industry-na-eu.md`, `scans/2026-07-france-cycle.md`, `scans/2026-07-spain-north-africa-pillars.md`, `scans/2026-07-spanish-cooperatives-ai.md` (EU industry and national cycles)

**Scope.** The EU institutional, funder, strategy and regulatory substrate for agrifood AI, re-verified at 2026-09-14 and written up as twelve units. Scope excludes: member-state AI industry deployments (covered by the France, Spain and regional-industry scans), non-EU institutional layers, and the farm-level deployment evidence layer.

**Method.** Two-phase recon-then-deep: eight parallel primary-source research passes (EIT Food; Horizon Europe Cluster 6 and Mission Soil; Copa-Cogeca and CEMA; the AI Act; the AI Office and GPAI Code; the CRCF; the AI Continent Action Plan and Apply AI Strategy; EAFRD and CAP Strategic Plans), each writing a recon file under `~/ai-agrifood/`, followed by single-writer unit drafting with per-unit YAML and cross-reference audits. Boundary cases were re-checked by the cycle author in the units; one subagent misattribution was caught and corrected in `units/eu-mission-soil-deal-for-europe-living-labs.md`. Pre-deploy build verification: green.

**Cutoff.** 2026-09-14.

## 1. Why this scan now

Three reasons, in order of weight.

**The leads formed one cycle, not thirteen decisions.** The July funder scan surfaced seven "future units" and the regulatory scan five more. The corpus's own registry synthesis notes the pattern: when open leads cluster by surfacing scan, they are usually already co-scoped. The EU leads were an entire institutional architecture — funder layer, industry-voice layer, strategy layer, regulatory layer — named but never unitised. That architecture was the largest single unwritten block in the corpus.

**The freshness clock had expired with a live consequence.** Every gap entry in the corpus carried "Last-searched: Jul 2026". In the EU regulatory layer that staleness became a factual error: the **Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744)** was in force from 27 July 2026 and moved the high-risk timeline the July scan recorded.

**The July framing needed testing.** The funder scan asserted that the EU substrate is "the most multi-layered" of the EU, US and China substrates and that "European agritech AI activity follows EU funding flow". Both claims are worth holding up against primary sources — and both need qualification, not rejection.

## 4. The funding-flow story, refreshed

The July scan asked where the EU money is. This cycle can answer with figures rather than categories:

| Instrument | Verified money | Horizon |
|---|---|---|
| EAFRD (rural development, second pillar) | **€95.5bn** for 2021-2027, incl. €8.1bn NGEU; €66bn in-plan EU contribution across 28 CAP Strategic Plans | to 2027 |
| Horizon Europe Cluster 6 | **€676.40m (2026) / €630.85m (2027)**; AI/digital-named topics ≈ **€58m in 2026** | 2027 is the last work programme of the MFF |
| AgData partnership (Agriculture of Data) | **€300m total (€90m EC + €210m member states)**, 61 partners, 22 countries | 2025-2032 |
| agrifoodTEF (Digital Europe) | **€30m EU co-funding** within a €60m project; 34 partners, 9 countries; 200+ AI services; 5,000-innovator target | since January 2023 |
| EU Mission Soil | **€81m of 2026 calls** (€33m single-stage + €48m two-stage), incl. **€9m for the AI-ready decision-support topic**; **€94.8m** planned for 2027 | 100 Living Labs by 2030 |
| EIT Food (EIT KIC) | **€125.3m for 2026-2028**; 65 startups per cohort; €3,000 travel grants, €20k-€50k validation awards | 2026-2028 |
| EuroHPC / AI Factories | **~€10bn** (whole EuroHPC 2021-2027 envelope); 19 AI Factories + 13 antennas being set up | ongoing |
| AI Gigafactories | **Call open: up to €10bn public + ~€20bn private expected**; per-project €100m-€800m public | deadline 12 November 2026; bulk from the 2028-2035 MFF |
| Apply AI agri-food flagship ("Agri-food AI platform") | **No budget attached** | Q1 2027 |
| CRCF | Voluntary; **zero certificates issued**; Buyers' Club first permanent-removal purchases targeted December 2026 | agricultural units H1 2028 |

**Reading.** The EU's agrifood-AI-relevant money is real but strung across instruments — a research programme, a partnership, a testing facility, a mission, an accelerator, a compute envelope, a strategy with no budget, and a voluntary carbon framework with no certificates. **No single instrument is an "agrifood AI programme", and the AI-labelled share of any one of them is small.** That is the funding-flow finding, and it is more precise than July's "where money flows tells you where activity is".

**The funder-layer corollary:** the practitioner-facing money (EAFRD, €66bn in-plan) is the least AI-specific; the AI-specific money (Cluster 6 topics, €58m/year) is the least farm-facing. The bridge instruments — agrifoodTEF, AKIS, the sectoral dialogue — are services and advice, not capital.

## 5. The constraint-side story, corrected

1. **The AI Act's high-risk timeline had moved.** Annex III: **2 December 2027**. Annex I product-embedded: **2 August 2028**. National sandboxes: **2 August 2027**. Article 50 transparency and **the start of enforcement**: live from **2 August 2026**.
2. **Agricultural AI is mostly not high-risk.** Annex III does not name agriculture or food; Annex III(2)'s critical-infrastructure wording excludes the food system; food-safety AI is not covered by Annex I legislation (Val, EJRR 2025). **No Annex III category touches agrifood.**
3. **The binding route is product safety.** AI in agricultural machinery is an **Article 6(1) safety-component** question, and the Omnibus moved **Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 from Annex I Section A to Section B**, creating a **sectoral route**: only Articles 6(1), 60a and 102-112 apply, with AI requirements folded into the Machinery Regulation by delegated act **by 2 August 2028**. Regulation (EU) No 167/2013 (agricultural and forestry vehicles) was already in Section B.
4. **Enforcement exists, activity does not.** No fines, no formal investigations, no model restrictions six weeks in; "technical compliance dialogues" are the AI Office's stated preference. **No agritech vendor compliance posture was found** — and no agrifood, farmer or agritech organisation appears among the ~190 signatories of the Article 50 transparency code.
5. **Governance: no agrifood seat anywhere.** The AI Office has no agrifood unit (its sectoral unit is A6 "AI in Health and Life Science"); the AI Board has no agrifood sub-group; the 60-member Scientific Panel contains no evident agrifood specialist; and the **174-member Advisory Forum contains no agrifood, farmer, forestry, fisheries or agritech organisation** — Copa-Cogeca and CEMA are not members. The register record (E04009, 178 entries) was screened in full.
6. **The CRCF is voluntary and non-operational.** Methodologies are now adopted (permanent removals February 2026; carbon farming July 2026), eight schemes have applied, **none recognised**, **zero certificates issued**. Carbon-farming AI demand currently comes from the regulation's uncertainty and tier-3 data requirements, the **EU Carbon Farming Database**, the **JRC iMRV** project and EU-funded MRV consortia — not from a certified market.

**The cycle's central structural finding:** *the EU's agrifood AI architecture is funder-heavy, strategy-heavy and constituency-light.* Money, programmes, methodologies, strategies and dialogues exist in quantity. What does not exist is an agrifood seat in AI governance, an AI position from the EU's largest farm federation, or a single compliance actor from the agritech sector. The instrument that binds European agrifood AI is **machinery safety law**; the instrument that funds it is **an agricultural policy with no AI budget line**; the instrument that would pay farmers for it is **a carbon framework with no certificates**.

## 6. Cluster-pattern observation, updated

July's LAC cycle proposed a possible eighth cluster pattern ("multilateral-institutional convening + venture-funded SaaS + foundation-model collaboration"). The EU architecture cycle tests the EU's own pattern and produces a sharper formulation for Europe:

**EU cluster-pattern candidate — "multi-layer institutional substrate with dispersed instruments and thin sectoral representation".** Components:

1. **A funder layer with no agrifood-AI programme** — EAFRD, Cluster 6, EIT Food, Mission Soil, TEFs, each funding agrifood AI as a subset of something else.
2. **An industry-voice layer split by interest** — CEMA with a documented AI dossier; Copa-Cogeca without one.
3. **A strategy layer that names agri-food but does not fund it** — the Apply AI flagship is unbudgeted.
4. **A regulatory layer that regulates machinery, not farming intelligence** — the sectoral route is the binding instrument.
5. **A market-creation layer that has not transacted** — the CRCF.
6. **Thin sectoral representation in the governance layer** — zero agrifood seats.

This is *not* the July funder scan's "most multi-layered of the three". The EU is **the most instrument-dispersed and the most procedural**, and the dispersion has a measurable cost: no actor can point to "the EU's agrifood AI policy", because there isn't one; there are eight instruments with eight decision calendars. Whether this pattern is distinct from the US (commercial-vendor-led with state-research support) and China (state-led with commercial follow-on) is now better evidenced than in July — the distinction is in *architecture*, not in *layering*.

## 8. New contested claims surfaced by this scan

- **C-351 (new):** "The EU funds agrifood AI at scale." *Contesting:* the AI-labelled money in the largest single relevant programme is ≈€58m/year inside a €676m programme; the agri-food flagship has no budget; the compute envelopes are not agrifood-specific. *Position:* scale is real at the instrument level (hundreds of millions across instruments) and small at the agrifood-AI-labelled level (tens of millions per year). Both statements are true and the pairing is the finding.
- **C-352 (new):** "European agrifood AI activity follows EU funding flow." *Contesting:* Copa-Cogeca's project portfolio has no AI-specific project; EIT Food's AI startups concentrate in one of six hubs; the largest agrifood MRV commercial actors (Agreena, Soil Capital) operate in pre-CRCF private programmes. *Position:* EU funding defines where *institutional* activity happens; commercial agrifood AI activity follows venture and corporate demand, and the two overlap only partially.
- **C-353 (new):** "The EU's agrifood AI architecture is multi-stakeholder." *Contesting:* the stakeholder pattern holds in the funder and dialogue layers (multi-actor requirements, sectoral dialogues, Living Labs) and fails in the governance layer (zero agrifood seats) and the market layer (a buyer club of corporate offtakers). *Position:* multi-stakeholder in design, sectorally absent in governance.
- **C-354 (new):** "AI-in-machinery compliance is now settled by the sectoral route." *Contesting:* the delegated act folding AI requirements into the Machinery Regulation is unadopted; harmonised standards are pending; the Article 6 classification guidelines remain draft. *Position:* the route is settled in principle, not in practice.
- **C-355 (new):** "The CRCF creates agricultural carbon-AI demand." *Contesting:* zero certificates, no recognised scheme, agricultural units expected in 2028. *Position:* the CRCF creates *methodological* demand today (uncertainty quantification, tier-3 data, MRV harmonisation) and market demand only from 2028.

## Why this scan matters for talks

- **"There is no EU agrifood AI policy"** is the cycle's headline finding, and it is defensible: eight instruments, eight calendars, no programme, no budget line, no governance seat.
- **The corrected AI Act dates** (Annex III 2 December 2027; Annex I 2 August 2028; sandboxes 2 August 2027) are mandatory for any regulatory talk.
- **"Agricultural AI is mostly not high-risk"** is counter-intuitive and sourced.
- **The machinery sectoral route** is the corpus's clearest sector-specific regulatory design choice — and it is agriculture-specific.
- **The absence finding**: 178 Advisory Forum entries screened, zero farming or agritech organisations. If the field guide has one fact to carry about EU agrifood AI governance, this is it.
- **The money map** (section 4) replaces the July scan's categories with figures, and shows the practitioner-facing money is the least AI-specific.
- **The zero-certificate carbon market** is the honest counterpoint to "carbon farming AI is booming".

## Critical context

- The AI Act content is fast-moving: the Omnibus is six weeks old at verification, the carbon-farming delegated act is not yet in force, and the Article 6 classification guidelines are draft. Treat every date in section 5 as a re-verification target, not a settled fact (G-436).
- Consumer-facing EU agrifood AI is thin in this cycle: the corpus's consumer and distribution cells remain under-populated on the EU side, and nothing here changes that.
- The "no agrifood seat" finding is a documented search result over a published register, not proof that no agrifood organisation applied.
- The cluster-pattern formulation in section 6 is an observation with one cycle behind it, not a typology. It needs testing against another institutional architecture (Japan's WAGRI, India's AgriStack, or the IICA-convened LAC layer).
- Two July figures (Mission Soil's 2025 call budgets) remain unverified rather than corrected (G-409).
