# AI and Labour — six strata, four inversions, and the discontinued cluster as labour-displacement negative result — July 2026

**Status.** Cycle substantive analytical consolidation. Modelled on the regional deepening scans' structure; differs in being a *spine* scan rather than a *regional* scan — reads across regions, value-chain stages, and actor types to surface the labour pattern that runs through the corpus. Built on the recon in `scouts/2026-07-ai-and-labour-scout.md`.

**Why this scan exists.** The existing corpus has ~30 units with explicit labour-side content (frontmatter `purpose: worker-conditions`, `critical-voice: worker-conditions`, or substantive labour discussion in body). They cluster in six strata — on-farm robotics, specialty-crop AI, food-processing AI, consumer-AI / discontinued cluster, critical voices, and labour-policy / succession / demographic — but no single scan has read them together as a *labour spine*. This scan does that. Per `talks/README.md` Status section, "AI and labour / succession" is named as a next-iteration spine candidate with anchor units lely-astronaut + naio-technologies + quotes/torrie-jeff-lely-succession — partial surfacing. This scan makes the spine load-bearing.

**Method.** Read existing units as primary sources for the labour-side content of each stratum; cross-reference Sullivan + Carolan + Guthman + Fairbairn + Mitchell + Friedland intellectual lineage as the analytical foundation; surface four structural inversions + two structural absences + two structural features as the analytically distinctive contributions; identify cluster-pattern-taxonomy candidate *labour-substitution-via-state-cluster*; track negative-finding-tolerance per METHOD #13 (Track 7 migration / seasonal labour + AI named as substantive corpus-thin territory).

**Cutoff.** Active in the last 24 months (July 2024 – July 2026). Older material (Lely 1992 origin, Sullivan ethnography 2021-2025, Sullivan intellectual lineage 1981-2025, Torrie 2018 quote) included only when load-bearing.

**Scope.** Global per user direction. Canadian anchor material (UFCW / NFU / CLC + AIDA dead + AI for All agriculture priority sector) is *substantively anchored* and ready for a future Canadian regional cycle — but is not the regional emphasis here. The Canadian institutional voice layer is anchored enough to warrant a dedicated unit within the global cycle.

## 1. The six labour strata — what each contains

The labour-side content of the corpus organises naturally into six strata. Each stratum has a distinct *deployment pattern*, a distinct *labour-displacement framing*, and a distinct *verification gap*.

### 1.1 Stratum 1 — On-farm robotics and autonomy

**What it is.** Robotic systems deployed at the field or animal level: dairy robotics (Lely, DeLaval, Shouguang), vineyard / specialty-crop robotics (Naïo, Wall-Ye, Vitirover), row-crop equipment-vendor autonomy (Deere See & Spray, CNH Driverless Tillage / Driver Assist Harvest / Trident 5550 / Baling Automation, AGCO PTx brand-agnostic retrofit), drone-based (DJI, XAG, Yamaha Fazer RMAX, Pinduoduo, Taranis), vertical / indoor (Spread, Ekonoke, Plenty, AeroFarms).

**Deployment scale (concrete numbers).**
- Lely Astronaut: 50,000+ robots installed across 50+ countries; ~3 million cows milked daily; first prototype 1992, commercially available from 1995, 50,000th unit installed at Hollybank Farm, County Antrim, Northern Ireland, July 2024. **The largest deployed agricultural robotics deployment by deployed scale.**
- Deere See & Spray: 5 million+ acres 2025 growing season (vendor-reported). **Largest deployed computer-vision-driven precision spraying deployment by acreage.**
- CNH Industrial: Driver Assist Harvest (commercially available for Case IH and New Holland), Driverless Tillage (announced 2022; commercial availability narrower than Deere See & Spray), Case IH Trident 5550 (announced as industry's first autonomous spreader, Farm Progress Show 2022).
- AGCO PTx: brand-agnostic retrofit that works on competitor equipment including Deere.

**Labour-displacement framing.** The CNH Neilson framing — *"The agriculture industry is faced with the challenges of time, costs, constrained operational windows, and a dwindling labor force. Autonomy will become an integral part of farmers' operations everywhere..."* — is the canonical equipment-vendor articulation that autonomy is the *strategic response* to labour decline. The Lely framing — *"By changing the type of work and making the work more pleasant, we help to contribute to succession"* (vendor) and *"If we didn't invest in new technology, there wasn't going to be succession"* (Torrie, Grey County ON farmer) — is the family-succession motivation that is *co-extensive* with labour-displacement but framed differently. The Naïo framing — *specialty-crop / vineyard robotics as small-farm viability* (COO Sarlandie, founder Matteo Vanotti) — is the *small-farmer-via-large-firm* pattern that the corpus has not previously named as a structural move.

**Verification gap.** Lely 50,000 robots is concrete deployed scale; what does the workforce look like at a 50,000-robot-deployment scale? Pre/post employment data is structurally thin across the stratum. The CNH Driverless Tillage announcement vs deployment-scale gap is named in the CNH-Raven unit ("announcements exceed deployments at every player except Deere See & Spray"). G-017 (US row-crop autonomous machinery operational scale beyond Deere) is partially addressed but not closed.

**Substantive unit candidate.** This stratum already has substantial coverage in existing units (lely-astronaut, naio-technologies, john-deere-see-and-spray, cnh-raven-autonomous-portfolio, agco-ptx, niqo-robotics-india, dji-agriculture-global-export, xag-china-drone-leader, spread-techno-farm-vertical-lettuce-japan, ekonoke-spanish-indoor-hop-hydroponics-ai). The cycle's contribution here is *consolidation* — a new unit is not required; the substantive analytical work is in the scan's reading-together.

### 1.2 Stratum 2 — Specialty-crop AI (the labour-shape inversion)

**What it is.** AI systems deployed in specialty-crop production: strawberry robotics (Advanced Farm Technologies Davis CA), intra-row weeding (FarmWise Vulcan San Francisco CA), laser-weeding (Carbon Robotics LaserWeeder Seattle WA), berry harvest prototype (Harvest CROO Plant City FL), orchard IPM (CropVue + OKSIR Surrey BC / Osoyoos BC), pineapple AI (PineSort / AInnovatech Costa Rica), Indian specialty-crop robotics (Niqo Robotics).

**Deployment scale (concrete numbers).**
- Advanced Farm Technologies strawberry robotics: **commercial scale since 2019**, Oxnard and Santa Maria California. Per Ochoa/Mo *MDPI Actuators* 14(2):42, 2025 (cited 14 times).
- FarmWise Vulcan: 3 acres/hour coverage; vendor-reported *"$250+ per acre"* saved; Taylor Farms customer reference; RDO Equipment Co. exclusive distribution partnership announced 2025.
- Carbon Robotics LaserWeeder: operational scale at multiple US farms; specific acreage + customer base data fragmented (G-168 substantive gap).
- Harvest CROO berry harvest: **prototype-only** — over a decade of development since 2013; commercial-scale not reached.
- Sullivan ethnography: head-lettuce harvest automation **at "zero percent"** (April 2025 quote from Salinas Valley agriculture automation expert).

**Labour-displacement framing.** The specialty-crop stratum is where the labour-displacement question is *most-contested*. The Sullivan "zero percent" head-lettuce finding is the direct counterweight to vendor and investor pitches that conflate progress on weeding with progress on harvest. *"While some farming tasks such as weeding and thinning are becoming increasingly digital in the Salinas Valley and elsewhere, harvesting of specialty crops remains the elusive 'holy grail' of automation"* (Sullivan SSRC Just Tech September 2025).

**The structural finding.** **Commercial-scale deployment is achieved for weeding; commercial-scale is not achieved for harvest.** This is a substantive distinction that any labour-spine talk should foreground. The labour-displacement question is *answered differently* for the same agrifood sector depending on which task is automated.

**The structural inversion.** Within the same cell (specialty-crop AI), weeding is *labour-replacing* at commercial scale, while harvest is *labour-requiring* — and Sullivan's field research documents that *"hiring farmworkers for the romaine lettuce harvest is still cheaper and more reliable than the existing automated harvesters"*. The labour-substitution pace is bounded by what the technology can do, not by what the vendor pitch promises.

**Verification gap.** Florida berry harvest, Washington apple harvest, Central Valley table grape harvest, Georgia Vidalia onion harvest labour-context are all under-surfaced (G-176 substantive; see proposed unit `units/us-specialty-crop-farmworker-context.md`). H-2A scale at the specialty-crop level is named in Sullivan unit only.

**Substantive unit candidate.** The advanced-farm-tech-farmwise-carbon-robotics-specialty-crop-weeders unit is the consolidated-cell anchor for the weeding commercial-scale layer. Sullivan is the consolidated-cell anchor for the harvest not-commercial-scale layer. The proposed new unit `units/us-specialty-crop-farmworker-context.md` closes G-176 substantively.

### 1.3 Stratum 3 — Food-processing AI (the labour-displacement surface)

**What it is.** AI systems deployed at the processing-line or plant-management level: Cargill CarVe (beef carcass vision, Fort Morgan CO + Friona TX + "others like it"); Tyson × AWS computer vision (poultry line-side); Smithfield vision + robotics (pork Denison IA + multi-plant); JBS USA × Völur (beef cutting-plan AI); KPM SiftAI (beef FM detection); Maple Leaf Foods (Braincube +12% yield / 3-month ROI; Emulate3D digital twin); SoraLINK × Saputo/Olymel/Agropur (predictive maintenance); Sysco AI360 (foodservice distribution); Winnow (foodservice kitchen waste AI).

**Deployment scale (concrete numbers).**
- Cargill CarVe: Fort Morgan CO $90 million automation investment; "27 billion pounds of beef annually" cumulative US scale against which yield improvements are measured; *"a one percent yield improvement can save hundreds of millions of pounds of meat"* (vendor framing).
- Tyson × AWS: poultry line-side CV at multiple plants (vendor framing).
- Smithfield: Denison IA flagship; multi-plant rollout (specific plant list not enumerated).
- Maple Leaf Foods: +12% yield / 3-month ROI / 100% golden batches (Braincube-reported) at Heritage Plant processed meats; >99% material-handling accuracy at London ON poultry plant (Emulate3D + Rockwell).
- SoraLINK: $250,000 saved in a single intervention (vendor-reported); predictive maintenance deployed across Saputo / Olymel / Agropur.

**Labour-displacement framing.** The processing stratum has the *most-substantive* labour-displacement framing in the corpus. Two structural features:

(a) **Process intelligence as a privacy surface.** *"The same camera that estimates yield can also be used to monitor individual worker pace, technique, breaks"* (Cargill CarVe critical context). Few public discussions have surfaced this dual-use implication; the technology is being deployed *to* workers, not only *for* yield reporting.

(b) **Concentration effect.** Cargill, Tyson, JBS USA, and National Beef together process the great majority of US fed cattle. *"If CarVe-class technology spreads across this oligopoly, the cumulative effect on yield, slaughter volume, and pricing is meaningful; if it stays proprietary to Cargill, the yield edge accrues to one firm."*

**The structural inversion.** Within the same cell (meat-processing AI), Cargill + Tyson + Smithfield + JBS deployments are *labour-monitoring + yield-improving* at the line-side, while Maple Leaf Foods Braincube is *digital-twin + plant-optimisation* — the same general "process intelligence" framing with structurally different unit economics. Smithfield has historically had worker-safety + union-relations criticism; the AI deployment is not framed as a worker-benefit technology. UFCW representation at meat-packing is the labour-organisation-side that the corpus has not surfaced.

**Verification gap.** Cargill is the source for all deployment evidence on CarVe; no independent third-party verification of yield improvements has surfaced. Smithfield does not enumerate every plant. Maple Leaf +12% yield / 3-month ROI is Braincube-reported (vendor), not independently verified. UFCW / UFCW Canada formal position on AI deployment in meat-processing not surfaced as primary source. Packer and Stockyards Act (1921, USDA) + AI deployment at scale is structurally absent.

**Substantive unit candidate.** The proposed new unit `units/labour-displacement-na-meat-processing.md` consolidates Cargill + Tyson + Smithfield + JBS + Maple Leaf + SoraLINK read against UFCW + Smithfield historical + Packer and Stockyards Act. Closes the labour-monitoring dual-use naming for all five (currently only Cargill CarVe names it).

### 1.4 Stratum 4 — Consumer-AI / hospitality and the discontinued cluster

**What it is.** AI systems deployed at the consumer-facing or back-of-house restaurant layer: Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen (in-store robotic salad makeline), Wendy's FreshAI × Google Cloud (voice AI drive-thru), Taco Bell × Omilia (voice AI drive-thru 890+ units), McDonald's × IBM AOT (voice AI drive-thru, **discontinued** June 2024), Kroger × Ocado CFC closures (**discontinued** November 2025), DoorDash voice AI (**discontinued**), Albertsons × Afresh (in-store fresh AI), Loblaw × Blue Yonder (forecasting), Walmart Sparky (consumer AI), Instacart cart assistant (consumer AI).

**Deployment scale (concrete numbers).**
- Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen: 18 of 37 net new restaurant openings fiscal 2025 + 7-9 new units + 2-4 retrofits; September 2025 sold the Spyce business to Wonder for $186.4 million while retaining deployment rights.
- Wendy's FreshAI: ~100 restaurants across 17 states (January 2025); 86% average order-completion rate without human intervention (vendor number); ~99% accuracy if measured by Wendy's broader definition (any AI-started order submitted to POS); 22 seconds faster than Columbus market average at one test site.
- Taco Bell × Omilia: 890+ drive-thru voice AI units.
- McDonald's × IBM AOT: 100+ restaurants in NA; **discontinued** June 2024.
- Kroger × Ocado CFC closures: Frederick MD, Goodyear AZ, third unreported; "bet on robotics went too far" (Grocery Dive November 19, 2025).
- DoorDash voice AI: **discontinued**.

**Labour-displacement framing.** The Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen unit names the labour-displacement dimension explicitly: *"Restaurant automation has a labour displacement dimension — Sweetgreen has not publicly committed to a no-layoffs policy tied to Infinite Kitchen deployment."* Wendy's FreshAI 86%-completion-without-human-intervention is the most-specific public labour-automation-rate metric on a voice-AI deployment. McDonald's × IBM AOT unit names worker/labour union concerns raised during the pilot period; *"the discontinuation may have been partly tactical (cost / accuracy) rather than a labour settlement."*

**The structural finding.** **Four high-profile US consumer-AI / hospitality robotics deployments walked back in an 18-month window.** McDonald's × IBM ended June 2024. DoorDash voice AI discontinued. Kroger × Ocado closures November 2025. Sweetgreen Spyce sold September 2025 (with deployment rights retained). This is structurally novel as an *industry pattern* — the discontinued cluster is the corpus's most-evidenced *labour-displacement-doesn't-happen-yet* layer.

**The structural inversion.** Within the same cell (consumer-AI / hospitality AI), Wendy's / Taco Bell / Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen are *labour-automation-targeted at working scale*, while McDonald's / Kroger / DoorDash / Sweetgreen-Spyce-as-divestment are *labour-automation-constrained by technology readiness + capital economics*. The labour-displacement question is *answered differently* for the same general restaurant-AI pattern depending on whether the deployment walked back or stayed.

**Verification gap.** Front-of-house worker voice on AI deployment is not surfaced as primary source. Restaurant Workers Union / SEIU / UNITE HERE formal position on consumer-AI labour is not surfaced. Back-of-house worker count + role-shift data in working consumer-AI deployments is not surfaced.

**Substantive unit candidate.** The proposed new unit `units/consumer-ai-discontinued-labour-displacement.md` consolidates Sweetgreen + McDonald's + Kroger + DoorDash as the labour-displacement-doesn't-happen-yet layer. The discontinued cluster is currently scattered across four units; reading them together is the structural move.

### 1.5 Stratum 5 — Critical voices

**What it is.** Substantive analytical voices on agrifood AI labour: Sullivan (UC Santa Cruz, PhD candidate; SSRC Just Tech September 2025; *Nature Food* 2025 co-author), Carolan (Colorado State, Professor of Sociology; *Agriculture and Human Values* 2024; *Sociologia Ruralis* 2023; *New Media & Society* 2023), Fairbairn + Kish + Guthman (*Pitching Agri-Food Tech* *J Cultural Economy* 2022), Guthman (*The Problem with Solutions* UC Press 2024), Mitchell (*Taylorism Comes to the Fields* *Economic Geography* 2023), Friedland + Barton + Thomas (*Manufacturing Green Gold* CUP 1981), Fairbairn et al. (*Digital Agriculture Will Perpetuate Injustice Unless Led from the Grassroots* *Nature Food* 2025).

**Why the critical-voice lineage matters.** The dominant voices in US agrifood AI discourse are vendors (Bayer, Deere, Indigo, AGCO), federal agencies (USDA AI Strategy), academic research institutes (AgAID, AIIRA, AIFARMS, AI-LEAF), and land-grant deployment (Cooperative Extension). What is *not* there is substantive sociological / labour-conditions / disability-and-recognition critique from US-based researchers. Sullivan + Carolan + the UCSC cluster fill that gap.

**The Sullivan intellectual lineage.** Per the Sullivan unit:
- **Madeleine Fairbairn, Zenia Kish, Julie Guthman** (UC Santa Cruz) — *"Pitching Agri-Food Tech: Performativity and Non-Disruptive Disruption in Silicon Valley,"* *Journal of Cultural Economy* 15(5), 2022.
- **Julie Guthman** — *The Problem with Solutions: Why Silicon Valley Can't Hack the Future of Food* (UC Press, 2024).
- **Tomás Almaguer** — *Racial Fault Lines: The Historical Origins of White Supremacy in California* (UC Press, 1994).
- **Richard Walker** — *The Conquest of Bread: 150 Years of Agribusiness in California* (New Press, 2004).
- **Don Mitchell** — *"Taylorism Comes to the Fields: Labor Control, Labor Supply, Labor Process, and the Twilight of Fordism in California Agribusiness,"* *Economic Geography* 99(4), 2023.
- **William H. Friedland, Amy Barton, Robert Thomas** — *Manufacturing Green Gold: Capital, Labor, and Technology in the Lettuce Industry* (CUP, 1981). The historical baseline for Salinas Valley labour and technology analysis.
- **Fairbairn et al.** — *"Digital Agriculture Will Perpetuate Injustice Unless Led from the Grassroots,"* *Nature Food* 6 (2025).

**The Carolan analytical position.** Carolan's *Agriculture and Human Values* 2024 paper *"Who and what gets recognized in digital agriculture"* is the most substantive recent US-engaged work on the *recognition* dimension — how digital agriculture's recognition structures (what counts, what is measured, whose work is visible) systematically exclude disabled workers, farmworkers with limited formal documentation, and care work. The (Dis)Ableism framing is structurally distinctive. *"Digital agriculture killjoy: Happy objects and cruel quests"* (Sociologia Ruralis 2023) names what Carolan calls the "happy objects" framing of AI / robotics / data tools and the "cruel quests" that follow when these framings meet labour realities on farms. *"The perilous promise of productivity: Affective politics"* (New Media & Society 2023) names the political economy of productivity claims in digital agriculture.

**The structural inversion.** Within the critical-voice stratum, Sullivan and Carolan are both *substantive* but operate at *different analytical levels*. Sullivan is *ethnographic* — field visits, interviews, focus groups in the Salinas Valley 2021-2025. Carolan is *sociological* — the broader recognition / disability / labour analytical frame. Together they form a complementary pair: Sullivan's empirical depth on Salinas Valley + Carolan's analytical breadth on US digital agriculture more broadly.

**Verification gap.** The UCSC critical-food-systems cluster is *not* unitised as an ecosystem map (per METHOD #6 four-layer pattern). The Carolan CSU cluster is *not* unitised as an ecosystem map. The `quotes/farmworkers/` category is named but never built. The Sullivan SSRC essay contains verbatim farmworker focus-group quotes + verbatim roboticist quotes that the cycle should land.

**Substantive unit candidates.** The proposed new unit `units/sullivan-guthman-fairbairn-mitchell-ucsc-cluster.md` is the Sullivan-Guthman-Fairbairn-Mitchell UCSC cluster ecosystem map per METHOD #6. The proposed new unit `units/carolan-colorado-state-ecosystem.md` is the parallel Carolan CSU cluster ecosystem map.

### 1.6 Stratum 6 — Labour-policy / state-cluster labour-substitution

**What it is.** State-led or state-stewarded labour-substitution patterns: Korea 30%-by-2027 smart-farming adoption target (RDA Smart Farm Innovation Valley; ioCrops; Daedong; Act on Fostering Smart Farm); Japan WAGRI state-stewarded DPI substrate + Yamaha Fazer RMAX industrial-automation heritage; Canada AI for All agriculture as priority sector (June 2026); EU AI Act labour-side implementation track; US labour-side federal regulatory context (USDA Packers and Stockyards Act; NLRB; Department of Labor).

**State-led labour-substitution patterns.**
- **Korea — explicit state-led labour-substitution target.** 30%-by-2027 smart-farming adoption; RDA Smart Farm Innovation Valley; ioCrops + Daedong + Act on Fostering Smart Farm. The Korean cluster is the *most-substantive* state-led labour-substitution corpus anchor because the target is explicit.
- **Japan — industrial-automation heritage + state-stewarded DPI.** Yamaha Fazer RMAX (motorcycle engine competence applied to agriculture); WAGRI state-stewarded data platform; Spread Techno vertical lettuce. The Japanese cluster is the *industrial-automation heritage* labour-substitution pattern.
- **Canada — adoption-led strategy, not regulation-led.** AI for All strategy June 2026 names agriculture as priority sector. *250,000 new AI jobs in five years*; AI adoption from ~12% to 60% by 2034; up to 90,000 work placements; ~$2B new programme spending. *No new binding AI rules* — the "trust" pillar promises new legislation on deepfakes, surveillance pricing, online safety, stronger privacy law, but none tabled as of July 2026.
- **EU — labour-side regulatory implementation track.** EU AI Act Title III high-risk AI systems; employment context is one of eight high-risk categories; AI Act enforcement body (European AI Office + national authorities) labour-side implementation is structurally distinct from data-governance track.

**Labour-side organised voices.** UFCW Canada (245,000+ food-processing members; submission to Senate AGFO committee on technology and labour); UFCW International (1.3M+ members); National Farmers Union Canada (Big Data in Canadian Agriculture SSHRC synthesis August 2021); Canadian Labour Congress (submission against AIDA 2023-2024); Coalition of Immokalee Workers (CIW Fair Food Program); United Farm Workers (UFW); PCUN (Oregon farmworkers); UFW Foundation; Farmworker Association of Florida; Familias Unidas por la Justicia (Washington); California Rural Legal Assistance.

**Verification gap.** Korean state-cluster labour-substitution outcome data (the 30%-by-2027 target has a labour-side outcome dimension — rural labour force, farm size, generational succession — not unitised). EU AI Act labour-side implementation track. US labour-side federal regulatory context. Canadian labour-side federal regulatory context (Temporary Foreign Worker Program / SAWP; ESDC; Service Canada).

**Substantive unit candidates.** The proposed new unit `units/korean-state-cluster-labour-substitution.md` consolidates the Korean cluster as the state-led labour-substitution anchor. The proposed new unit `units/ufcw-nfu-clc-canada-labour-producers.md` consolidates the Canadian labour-side institutional voice layer (closes G-115 substantively).

## 2. The four structural inversions

The labour-spine's analytically distinctive contribution is the *structural inversions* — same technology, opposite empirical outcomes. The inversions are *load-bearing* for any labour-spine talk because they prevent the dominant vendor / investor pitch from being the only framing.

### 2.1 Inversion A — Harvest vs weeding split

Within the same agrifood sector (US specialty-crop AI), weeding is automated at commercial scale (Advanced Farm Technologies strawberry 2019; FarmWise Vulcan 2025; Carbon Robotics LaserWeeder operational; Harvest CROO decade-plus prototype for berry harvest) while harvest is not (Sullivan "zero percent" head-lettuce April 2025; romaine field-pack labour process 14 farmworkers / 7 rows / 2 per row). *"Hiring farmworkers for the romaine lettuce harvest is still cheaper and more reliable than the existing automated harvesters"* (Sullivan).

The labour-displacement question is *answered differently* for the same agrifood sector depending on which task is automated. **The labour-spine talk must hold both without collapsing either.**

### 2.2 Inversion B — Succession vs displacement

Within the same technology (Lely Astronaut robotic milking), the framing is "family-farm succession" (Torrie, Grey County ON farmer: *"If we didn't invest in new technology, there wasn't going to be succession. That's what it came down to."*) by farmers and "work-life balance" by vendors (Cargill Lely: *"By changing the type of work and making the work more pleasant, we help to contribute to succession"*). Both are real. The labour-displacement question and the labour-succession question are *co-extensive* in dairy robotics but framed differently. The same applies to Naïo Technologies — specialty-crop / vineyard robotics framed as *small-farmer viability* by French cooperative discourse (Pillaud framing) and as *labour-cost reduction* by vendors (COO Sarlandie).

The labour-spine talk must hold both framings without collapsing either. The vendor framing and the producer framing are not in tension; they are different lenses on the same labour-displacement event.

### 2.3 Inversion C — State-led vs organic labour-substitution

Within the same labour-substitution question (who works on farms in 2035?), the *pattern* is structurally different:
- **State-led + quantitative target** (Korea 30%-by-2027 smart-farming adoption; Japan WAGRI state-stewarded DPI substrate).
- **Vendor-led + organic deployment** (Deere See & Spray 5M+ acres 2025; CNH Driverless Tillage; Lely 50,000 robots; Naïo 300+ robots).

The labour-substitution question is the same, but the substitution *pattern* is structurally different — state-led + quantitative target vs vendor-led + organic deployment.

**Cluster-pattern-taxonomy candidate.** *labour-substitution-via-state-cluster* is a substantive cluster-pattern candidate distinct from the existing cluster-with-state-substrate (Argentine beef AI SENASA + SIGSA) and cluster-with-state-strategy (UAE ADAFSA dual-mode). The Korean 30%-by-2027 + Japanese industrial-automation-heritage + Chinese WAICO multilateral posture could form a state-led labour-substitution cluster pattern. **The Korean state-cluster labour-substitution unit substantiates this candidate.**

### 2.4 Inversion D — Discontinued cluster as labour-displacement negative result

Within the same consumer-AI / hospitality AI pattern (Wendy's / Taco Bell / Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen / McDonald's × IBM / Kroger × Ocado / DoorDash / Sweetgreen Spyce-as-divestment), the *intent* (cost reduction, automation, scaling) was explicit, but the labour-displacement outcome was bounded by *technology readiness* and *capital economics*. McDonald's × IBM AOT ended June 2024. DoorDash voice AI discontinued. Kroger × Ocado closures November 2025 ("bet on robotics went too far"). Sweetgreen Spyce sold September 2025 (with deployment rights retained). **Where the technology wasn't ready, labour displacement didn't happen.**

The empirical record is more nuanced than the dominant vendor / investor pitch. The labour-displacement question is *answered differently* depending on whether the deployment walked back or stayed.

## 3. Two structural absences

### 3.1 Absence A — Labour-monitoring / surveillance dual-use

The Cargill CarVe unit explicitly names the dual-use: *"the same camera that estimates yield can also be used to monitor individual worker pace, technique, breaks."* This is the only unit in the corpus that names the surveillance dimension explicitly.

The Tyson × AWS poultry CV, Smithfield vision + robotics, Wendy's FreshAI × Google Cloud 86%-completion-without-human-intervention, and Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen makeline have the same dual-use structure but their units do not name it. The labour-spine must surface the surveillance dimension as a substantive analytical move — *process intelligence as a privacy surface* is the analytical framing, not just an implementation detail.

**Proposed cycle work.** The proposed unit `units/labour-displacement-na-meat-processing.md` closes this absence by naming labour-monitoring dual-use for all five meat-processing AI units and by elevating the surveillance dimension as a substantive analytical move. G-3XX (new): labour-monitoring / surveillance dual-use of agrifood computer vision systems.

### 3.2 Absence B — Labour-displacement quantification

Lely 50,000 robots is concrete deployed scale; what does the workforce look like at a 50,000-robot-deployment scale? Pre/post employment data is structurally thin across the corpus. Same for Deere 5M+ acres See & Spray 2025; CNH Driverless Tillage / Driver Assist Harvest; Wendy's FreshAI 86%-completion-without-human-intervention; Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen 18 new restaurants FY2025.

The deployment-scale numbers are well-documented (the corpus has them); the workforce-impact numbers are not. The labour-spine cannot answer the quantification question from existing sources — it must name the quantification gap as a substantive analytical move.

**Proposed cycle work.** The proposed units `units/labour-displacement-na-meat-processing.md` + `units/consumer-ai-discontinued-labour-displacement.md` + the Korean state-cluster unit foreground the workforce-impact gap. G-3XX (new): labour-displacement quantification in NA meat processing (Cargill + Tyson + Smithfield + JBS deployments; what does the workforce look like pre/post?). G-3XX (new): back-of-house worker displacement quantification in working consumer-AI deployments.

## 4. Two structural features

### 4.1 Feature A — Labour-side organised voice is thin
