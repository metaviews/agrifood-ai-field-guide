# EU architecture + China primary-document cycles — synthesized findings

**Status.** Cycle-level consolidation of substantive findings for talk-building, covering the two September 2026 cycles (EU architecture: 12 units closing the thirteen July EU leads; China primary-document: 14 units from Chinese-language primary sources) and the November 2026 refresh-and-discovery pass (14 units refreshed in place, 3 new units: `edic-agri-food.md`, `china-agricultural-llms-landscape.md`, `china-state-farms-provincial-deployment.md`). Written 15 November 2026. Each unit carries its own "Why this matters" material; the two cycle scans carry "Why this scan matters for talks" sections. **This document collapses those into a single navigable index: which findings are the headline arguments, and which archetypes they upgrade.**

**What changed in these cycles.** Before September, the corpus held EU material as thirteen open leads and China material as an English-language secondary-source picture. After September and November: the EU institutional architecture is a unitised block with corrected AI Act dates and verified funding figures, and China is a document-chain-and-deployment-vehicle picture from primary Chinese sources — the first jurisdiction in the corpus where the state's own instruments are the primary evidence rather than context.

**The cross-block synthesis (the headline of this document).** The EU and China are the corpus's two completed institutional-architecture pictures, and they are near-mirror images. The EU is instrument-rich, constituency-light, enforcement-just-starting: eight instruments, eight decision calendars, no agrifood AI programme, no agrifood seat in AI governance, and money that is real but dispersed. China is instrument-poor at the sector level and state-rich in deployment: AI arrives inside existing instruments (the No. 1 Central Document, the Smart Agriculture Action Plan, MIIT factory tiering) rather than as a standalone strategy, and the state instrumented what it owns (grain reserves, offshore platforms, state farms, its own data estate) before it instrumented what farmers do. **Neither jurisdiction has an "agrifood AI policy" in the sense audiences assume — the EU has instruments without a programme; China has a programme without a standalone instrument.** That pairing is the strongest comparative frame the corpus now holds, and it is the natural spine for any policy-audience talk built from these two cycles.

## Part I — the EU architecture block (12 units + EDIC)

### Finding EU-1. "There is no EU agrifood AI policy" (headline for regional-cluster-comparison and adoption-diagnosis)

Eight instruments, eight decision calendars, no programme, no budget line, no governance seat. Defensible from primary sources: the AI Act, the Machinery Regulation, the GPAI Code of Practice, the AI Continent Action Plan / Apply AI Strategy, Horizon Europe Cluster 6, the AgData partnership, EAFRD/CAP Strategic Plans, and the CRCF each run their own calendar; the one instrument that names agrifood AI adoption as a flagship (Apply AI's "Agri-food AI platform", Q1 2027) has no budget attached. Source: `scans/2026-09-eu-substrate-deepening.md` §5; units `eu-ai-continent-action-plan.md`, `eu-ai-act-agrifood-implications.md`.

### Finding EU-2. Agrifood holds no seat in EU AI governance (headline for critical-lens and regional-cluster-comparison)

The AI Office has no agrifood unit (its sectoral unit is A6 "AI in Health and Life Science"); the AI Board has no agrifood sub-group; the 60-member Scientific Panel contains no evident agrifood specialist; and the 174-member Advisory Forum contains no agrifood, farmer, forestry, fisheries or agritech organisation — Copa-Cogeca and CEMA are not members. The Transparency Register record (E04009, 178 entries) was screened in full. This is a documented search result over a published register, not proof that no agrifood organisation applied — say it that way. **If the talk carries one EU governance fact, carry this one.** Sources: `units/european-ai-office-governance-architecture.md` (G-427..G-430), scan §5.5.

### Finding EU-3. Agricultural AI is mostly not high-risk; the binding route is machinery safety law (headline for vendor-sweep and policy audiences)

Annex III does not name agriculture or food; Annex III(2)'s critical-infrastructure wording excludes the food system; food-safety AI is not high-risk (Val, EJRR 2025). What binds: AI in agricultural machinery is an Article 6(1) safety-component question, and the Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026) moved the Machinery Regulation from Annex I Section A to Section B — a sectoral route where only Articles 6(1), 60a and 102-112 apply, with AI requirements folded into the Machinery Regulation by delegated act due 2 August 2028. **Corrected dates, mandatory for any regulatory talk:** Article 50 transparency + enforcement live from 2 August 2026; national sandboxes 2 August 2027 (amended Article 57(1)); Annex III high-risk 2 December 2027; Annex I product-embedded high-risk 2 August 2028; Machinery Regulation applies 20 January 2027. Sources: `units/eu-ai-act-agrifood-implications.md`, `units/eu-machinery-regulation-agricultural-ai.md`, scan §3 + §5.

### Finding EU-4. The funding-flow map, in figures (headline for adoption-diagnosis and cooperative-alternative)

EAFRD €95.5bn 2021-2027 (incl. €8.1bn NGEU; €66bn in-plan EU contribution across 28 CAP Strategic Plans) — the practitioner-facing money, and the least AI-specific. Horizon Europe Cluster 6 €676.40m (2026) / €630.85m (2027), with AI/digital-named topics ≈ €58m in 2026 — the AI-specific money, and the least farm-facing. AgData partnership €300m (€90m EC + €210m member states, 61 partners, 22 countries). agrifoodTEF €30m EU co-funding (34 partners, 9 countries, 200+ AI services, 5,000-innovator target). Mission Soil €81m of 2026 calls incl. €9m for the AI-ready decision-support topic (€94.8m planned 2027; 100 Living Labs by 2030). EIT Food €125.3m for 2026-2028 (65 startups per cohort, six named hubs). EuroHPC ~€10bn envelope (19 AI Factories + 13 antennas). AI Gigafactories: call for up to €10bn public + ~€20bn expected private (€100m-€800m per project). Apply AI agri-food flagship: no budget. **The corollary is the talk line: the bridge instruments between farm-facing money and AI-specific money (agrifoodTEF, AKIS, sectoral dialogue) are services and advice, not capital.** Sources: scan §4; units `eafrd-cap-strategic-plans-digital-agriculture.md`, `horizon-europe-cluster-6-food-bioeconomy-agriculture.md`, `eit-food-eu-knowledge-innovation-community.md`, `eu-mission-soil-deal-for-europe-living-labs.md`.

### Finding EU-5. Enforcement moved from dialogue to named-company action (November 2026 refresh; mandatory freshness for any regulatory talk)

1 September 2026: first formal Article 91 information requests to 30+ companies (safety, security, copyright, transparency). 14 October 2026: EVP Virkkunen announced information requests to OpenAI and Anthropic by name, after the AI Office judged voluntary Code-of-Practice disclosures insufficient — the first company-named enforcement step. Still no fines and no formal investigations (G-213 updated but standing as of 2026-11-08). 12 October 2026: the first AI Act harmonised standard published — EN 18286:2026 (AI quality-management systems; Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/2274, OJ L 14.10.2026), giving high-risk providers a presumption of conformity with Article 17 QMS requirements and starting the standards pipeline that determines how machinery safety-component obligations become operable for agrifood equipment. The gigafactory call slipped twice (Lot 2 to 3 December 2026; Lot 1 to 4 February 2027) with all draft and submitted tenders discarded for resubmission — the slip pattern is itself evidence against the call-as-programme reading. Sources: `units/european-ai-office-governance-architecture.md`, `units/eu-ai-act-agrifood-implications.md`, `units/eu-machinery-regulation-agricultural-ai.md`, `units/eu-ai-continent-action-plan.md` (all refreshed 2026-11).

### Finding EU-6. EDIC for Agri-Food — the sector's first EU digital-infrastructure consortium, and its first mission is a Digital Farm ID (headline for data-sovereignty and regional-cluster-comparison)

Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1978 of 8 September 2026; statutory seat Paris; ten full members (Austria, Croatia, Finland, France, Italy, Netherlands, Poland, Romania, Slovenia, and the region of Flanders — the corpus's first subnational government as full member of an EU digital-infrastructure body) plus four observers (Germany, Ireland, Latvia, Spain). First named mission: a common **Digital Farm ID** — a federated, interoperable farm identifier interconnecting with national systems, aligned with the European Digital Identity Ecosystem, aimed at cutting reporting burden and enabling "secure access to high-quality data for AI-based services". The AI clause names risk management, traceability and carbon certification as candidate multi-country deployment actions, building on CEADS. **This is the first EU-level actor whose founding instrument literally names the Apply AI agrifood agenda** — and the most concrete EU-level farm-data-identity instrument to date: not a data space, not a registry, but an identifier layer that presupposes both. Data-rights design (farmer-controlled access rules vs. re-used national IACS identity asymmetries) is undocumented (G-475). Source: `units/edic-agri-food.md`.

### Finding EU-7. The zero-certificate carbon market (honesty handle against "carbon farming AI is booming")

CRCF: four certified activity categories; methodologies adopted (permanent removals February 2026; carbon farming July 2026); eight schemes applied, none recognised, zero certificates issued. Buyers' Club first permanent-removal purchases targeted December 2026; agricultural units expected H1 2028; Union registry December 2028. Carbon-farming AI demand today is methodological (uncertainty quantification, tier-3 data, the EU Carbon Farming Database, JRC iMRV) — not market demand (C-355). Source: `units/eu-carbon-removal-certification-framework.md`, scan §5.6.

### Finding EU-8. The EU cluster-pattern candidate (regional-cluster-comparison spine material)

**"Multi-layer institutional substrate with dispersed instruments and thin sectoral representation."** Six components: a funder layer with no agrifood-AI programme; an industry-voice layer split by interest (CEMA has an AI dossier, Copa-Cogeca does not — and its 22M-farmers figure is contested by its own 2018 publication's 13M); a strategy layer that names agri-food but does not fund it; a regulatory layer that regulates machinery, not farming intelligence; a market-creation layer that has not transacted; and zero agrifood seats in governance. The distinction from the US (commercial-vendor-led with state-research support) and China (state-led with commercial follow-on) is in *architecture*, not *layering* — the July framing ("most multi-layered of the three") is superseded. One cycle behind this observation; it needs testing against another institutional architecture (WAGRI, AgriStack, or the IICA-convened LAC layer). Sources: scan §6; `units/copa-cogeca-eu-farmer-cooperative-federation.md`, `units/cema-eu-agricultural-machinery-association.md`.

### Contested claims to carry (EU): C-351..C-355

C-351 "The EU funds agrifood AI at scale" (true at instrument level, small at agrifood-AI-labelled level — the pairing is the finding); C-352 "activity follows EU funding flow" (institutional activity yes; commercial activity follows venture/corporate demand); C-353 "the architecture is multi-stakeholder" (in design yes, in governance no); C-354 "machinery compliance is settled by the sectoral route" (in principle, not in practice — delegated act unadopted, standards pending, guidelines draft); C-355 "the CRCF creates carbon-AI demand" (methodological now, market from 2028).

## Part II — the China primary-document block (14 units + 2 November units)

### Finding CN-1. China instrumented its agriculture from the state inward (headline for regional-cluster-comparison)

Across all cycle units the recurring pattern is state capacity seeking visibility over its own stocks and flows: the grain reserve got AI first and most completely (automated intake inspection, the 稷元 storage model with 200,000+ knowledge units hosted on SASAC's AI platform, 21-day grain-condition forecasting, >4m tonnes through 80 enterprises); offshore aquaculture platforms are instrumented at construction with data registered as state-recognised assets; the national data programme measures its own estate (38 PB capacity, 18 PB stored, 105 TB open data across 4,673 datasets, 240 million tokens consumed annually) rather than farm adoption. **The corollary for talks: the corpus has almost no evidence of Chinese farmers using agricultural AI, and the one number that speaks to it — 240m tokens/year against roughly 200m farm households — suggests the on-farm layer is thin.** Sources: scan §4; `units/china-grain-storage-ai-jiyuan.md`, `units/china-mara-agricultural-data-resources-2026.md`, `units/china-deep-sea-smart-aquaculture-platforms.md`.

### Finding CN-2. AI arrives inside existing instruments, not as a new programme (headline for regional-cluster-comparison; the structural contrast with the US and EU)

The 2026 No. 1 Central Document folds AI into "new quality productive forces" as one clause alongside drones, IoT and robots — an authorisation, not a budget. The Smart Agriculture Action Plan (2024-2028, 农市发〔2024〕4号) folds it into extension and platforms (30% informatisation by 2026, 32% by 2028; three public-service products including a base-model open platform with a mandated open-source agricultural model community). MIIT folds it into smart-factory tiering (AI scenarios ≥30% excellent / ≥60% leading). There is no Chinese "AI in agriculture strategy" in the USDA or EU sense. The document chain is citable: seven instruments with dates, numbers and departments — the most concrete policy evidence the corpus holds for any jurisdiction outside the EU. Sources: scan §8; `units/china-no1-central-document-2026-ai.md`, `units/china-smart-agriculture-action-plan-2024-2028.md`, `units/china-digital-village-plan-2026-2030.md`, `units/china-food-processing-smart-factories.md`.

### Finding CN-3. State farms are the corpus's largest single agri-AI deployment vehicles (headline for regional-cluster-comparison and AI-and-labour)

Beidahuang: ~48M mu of farmland; its own information company running 智壤 coomap / 离朱 remote-sensing cloud / 耘图 planting-management platforms (99 algorithms integrated, ~3,000 datasets, 48,000+ GIS layer services, 19 agricultural systems); 48 satellites completing reclamation-wide staple-crop monitoring in 2 hours; a smart-equipment platform covering 111 farms with 84,000 connected units; smart irrigation on 54 farms with 19,000 automated gates (10-20% water saving); unmanned-farm operations at 万亩 scale with paddy/dryland labour reduced 50.3%/51.5%; the 北大荒未来农场 model piloted over 15,000 mu (Bawuwu soybean +5% yield, −5% fertiliser). Heilongjiang was the first province to issue a smart-agriculture implementation plan through the provincial government office, with Beidahuang and Jiamusi as model zones. **All figures are company/provincial self-reports with no audit and no regulated disclosure (G-481); the XPCC section is honestly thin — division-level cases only, no corps-level programme document found (G-479).** Sources: `units/china-state-farms-provincial-deployment.md`, `units/china-ground-robotics-unmanned-farms.md`.

### Finding CN-4. The agricultural-LLM landscape: a university-model wave, an SOE wave, and the corpus's first named Chinese farmer-level adoption (headline for open-source-smallholder and vendor-sweep)

Shennong 神农 (CAU) v4.0 July 2026 — pest/disease recognition 615 → 1,016 species at >93%; a September 2026 provincial-media feature claims all 31 provinces, "a million farmers", 5M+ mu — against >100k users at the 3.0 launch eleven months earlier, a discontinuity no source explains (G-477). People's Daily (January 2026) supplies the corpus's first named farmer-level adoption case in China: Du Lianhui, 600 mu of corn near Shenyang, per-mu cost down from ~480 to below 400 CNY; a WeChat mini-program for photo-based diagnosis; 36 specialist agents; the team's own admission that compute limits prevent unlimited farmer use. Sinong 司农 (NAU, January 2026): open-weight 8B/32B on ModelScope and GitHub — the only directly verifiable open-weights claim in the population. Tiangong Kaiwu 天工开悟 (HIT): first officially registered (备案) agricultural LLM, 24 crops / 64 varieties, deployed on 114 Heilongjiang farms serving 1M+ mu with claimed +5% yield / −10% cost (all HIT-reported). Gengyun 耕云 is the cautionary case: launched 2023 with iFlytek, no findable public entry point since. No benchmark, third-party audit or peer-reviewed outcome study of any named model exists (G-476). Sources: `units/china-agricultural-llms-landscape.md`, `units/china-smart-fisheries-fanli-llm.md`.

### Finding CN-5. The corrections are usable as method (headline for vendor-sweep — the vendor-report-hygiene demonstration)

Three claims in Western circulation survived only until someone read a Chinese primary source: **"China is drone-saturated"** — the sector's own co-founder says ~8% of farmland has drones attached (against a fleet of 300,000+); **"XAG holds more than half of Chinese agri-drone sales"** — contradicted by XAG's own HK-prospectus-commissioned Frost & Sullivan data (20.8% share; 18% in agri-robotics), the cycle's only V2 regulated-disclosure dataset; **"DJI's environmental totals"** — DJI's two 2026 documents disagree with each other (410 vs 480 Mt water; 51 vs 61.32 Mt CO₂; no methodology, no as-of date; G-033 closed as vendor-stated only). Plus: "TTA" and "Anyvision" do not exist as actors (verified competitors: EAVISION, 无锡汉和, 深圳天鹰兄弟, 珠海羽人, 安阳全丰); "certified pilots" are manufacturer-issued certificates under the joint CAAC/MARA training rule, not CAAC licences; the "Rural Revitalization 2027 Plan" was a consultancy artefact, superseded by the real Digital Village Plan 2026-2030 (CAC/MARA/MIIT, 11 September 2026). **That is the argument for the corpus's verification tiers in one slide.** Sources: scan §3 + §7; `units/xag-china-drone-leader.md`, `units/dji-agriculture-global-export.md`, `units/china-agricultural-drone-drift-liability.md`.

### Finding CN-6. The verification posture: government-primary but unaudited (method spine for any talk using Chinese figures)

Most cycle figures come from ministries, state enterprises or national institutes — better provenance than vendor press, still unaudited. V2 (regulated disclosure): XAG's prospectus only. V1 (peer-reviewed or official with named figures): HABLer (Computers and Electronics in Agriculture; 0.87 mean frame accuracy, ~60% less expert annotation time), the SCAU pig-factory review, Sinograin/稷元, DJI effectiveness via Food Policy 2025. V0 (institution- or vendor-reported): Muyuan, Wens AI4S, Fanli, DJI environmental totals, the processing claims, the Digital Village indicators, Beidahuang's estate figures. No independent evaluation of any Chinese agricultural AI system was located anywhere in the cycle. Say "government-reported" on every slide that carries these numbers. Sources: scan §7 + §11.

### Finding CN-7. Chinese evidence on the labour-substitution question is mixed by design (headline for AI-and-labour)

One unit cuts both ways on C-326: Muyuan replaces repetitious barn work wholesale (30+ smart devices per pig lifetime; 1.61m IoT devices connected, 1.04m online daily; >60m head served); XAG's drone labour is substituted through service providers rather than displacing farm workers; the deep-sea platforms' ~70% labour-cost saving has no disclosed baseline; Mengniu's Ningxia plant (~100 staff, 1m tonnes) supports the processing-automation claim; and no employment series exists either way. Beidahuang's unmanned-farm labour reductions (50.3%/51.5%) are self-reported. The ground-robotics unit's substantive correction sharpens all of it: **the binding constraint on Chinese field robotics is land consolidation and infrastructure — documented in MARA's own promoted-technology precondition clauses — not AI capability.** The *labour-substitution-via-state-cluster* pattern candidate (Korea 30%-by-2027 + Japan industrial-automation heritage + China WAICO posture) now has China-side deployment evidence, but China's version runs through state farms and land structure, not through a statutory adoption target. Sources: scan §10; `units/muyuan-smart-pig-farming.md`, `units/china-deep-sea-smart-aquaculture-platforms.md`, `units/china-ground-robotics-unmanned-farms.md`, `units/china-state-farms-provincial-deployment.md`.

### Finding CN-8. Mandated commons is not community commons (headline for open-source-smallholder-multilateral)

The open-source agricultural model community is mandated by a ministry plan (the Action Plan's base-model open platform), not emergent from developers. Sinong's open-weight 8B/32B release is verifiable; Fanli's public smart-fisheries dataset (116,000 image frames, 698,000 annotations) was released open under FAO auspices — the corpus's strongest Chinese open-data artefact. **Commons by mandate is a different object from commons by community; the open-source thread should compare them explicitly rather than treating both as open.** This complicates the Mozilla 89%-East-Asia-adoption reading productively: state strategy is a driver of that adoption figure. Sources: scan §10; `units/china-smart-agriculture-action-plan-2024-2028.md`, `units/china-smart-fisheries-fanli-llm.md`, `units/china-agricultural-llms-landscape.md`.

### Finding CN-9. The China cluster pattern, now with structure (regional-cluster-comparison spine material)

Six components, all primary-sourced: (1) state-directed umbrella with named instruments (No. 1 Document → Action Plan → Digital Village Plan → 数据要素× → MIIT tiering); (2) state-farm and state-enterprise deployment vehicles (Beidahuang, Sinograin, COFCO, provincial platform operators); (3) provincial variation as the implementation unit (Zhejiang, Heilongjiang, Chongqing, Guangdong, Liaoning); (4) state-adjacent research infrastructure as a distinct layer (CAU's National Digital Fisheries Innovation Centre, CAAS's HABLer/HERD, Sinograin's Chengdu institute — the institutions that write the standards and hold the data platforms); (5) a closed commercial platform layer underneath (Muyuan, Yangxiang, Nxin, JD/Tencent — "data islands" is the state's own word for the problem); (6) hardware-OEM export as the international channel (DJI, XAG, EAVISION through dealers; one named corporate JV, XAG × Charoen Pokphand; no platform export; the Belt-and-Road agritech-export framing did not hold as such). Plus the multilateral reach (WAICO, 29 founding states). Sources: scan §6; `units/china-agricultural-drone-drift-liability.md`, `units/chinese-agritech-belt-and-road-export.md`, `units/waico-alliance-china-multilateral-ai.md`.
